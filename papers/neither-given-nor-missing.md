# Neither Given nor Missing — the meaning *of* life, and why the other answers satisfy less than they first appear (11.05.26) vA

**Folder:** Connection Theology (CT) / Focus - Theology

It deepens a conversation of the same evening that began with his observation that CT now answers what is meaningful *in* life but says little about the meaning *of* life. **Lane:** the sentence *Meaning is neither given nor missing* is ruled, 11-5, by delegation ("your pick on the wording..."), the same way the shelf's #6 was ruled; it sits one found word away from true by meaning (§2). **What this is:** CT's answer to "what is the meaning of life?" — the *of* question, not the *in* question — in one sentence with its warrant; an account of what would make an answer to that question actually satisfying, as distinct from comforting; and a close look at eight of the answers people most often give, each at its best, each on its first look and its second, with what each gets right and what CT keeps of it.

## 0. The sentence

> **Meaning is neither given nor missing.**

Said of a life: *the meaning of life is neither given nor missing.* Nobody confers meaning on a life — not God, not a society, not a parent, not posterity, and not the person whose life it is. And no life lacks it. Its positive partner is already ruled: *I am where meaning occurs* (10-21). And what to do with it is John's two-line form: *contextualize in context; seek God.*

"Given" here means *conferred* — bestowed by someone's say-so. It does not mean "a given" in the sense of a plain fact; in that sense meaning is exactly a given, and that is the point.

## 1. What the question asks

John, 11-5: "what is the meaning OF life.  what does that question even mean?  is it something that could be answered?"

**The record first.** CT has three passes at meaning: *What You're For* (10-8), *What Meaning Is* (10-14) and the 10-31 hike note. The hike note's first compact line answered the *of* question in so many words — "The meaning of life is to deepen one's particular experience of what matters, recognizing that one's perspective does not confer its significance" — and by its last version had narrowed to *lived meaning*: what makes a life go well from inside. So John's sense is accurate to the record's drift. The corpus started at *of* and settled on *in*. This paper goes back to *of*.

**Four questions in one.** "What is the meaning of life?" bundles four different questions, and most confusion about it comes from answering one while being asked another.

1. *Purpose* — what is life **for**? This one presupposes someone or something life is for.
2. *Significance* — does any of it **matter**, given how large and old the universe is and how small and brief a life?
3. *Point* — what makes a life **go well**? This is the *in* question; the 10-31 note answers it.
4. *Story* — what does it all **add up to**? Is there a plot, an ending that makes sense of the middle?

**Four questions under those.** Beneath the four, when a person actually asks, there are usually four quieter ones, and they are what an answer has to meet if it is to be more than clever:

- *Do I matter?*
- *Can it be taken from me?*
- *Is it up to me to earn it, or make it?*
- *Am I alone in it?*

**Can it be answered?** Each of the four gets a different kind of answer, and CT has one for each:

1. *Purpose from outside: no.* A *for* is a relation to something other, and there is nothing other than the whole. That is the shape of "why is there anything," which the spine answers with no (`00 - START HERE` vW §2). And the whole wields nothing (the lemma, ruled 10-27; shelf #1), so it assigns nothing. The guard is the spine's guard, extended: the answer is *no*, never *pointless*. "Pointless" keeps the outside as the place a point would have to come from, and then reports it empty.
2. *Significance: yes, at its size.* What rides on you is real and not conferred (*What Meaning Is* §1). The sense of cosmic smallness is the scale discount, which is partiality — a theorem (`discount_by_scale_is_partial`).
3. *Point:* the 10-31 formulation — deepen your particular experience of what matters, knowing your perspective does not confer its significance. CT gives the form and never the content (*What Meaning Is* §2).
4. *Story:* no cosmic plot. A life's account of itself is graded like any account; the ledger is not closed by a story (*What Meaning Is* §6).

So the *of* question is answerable. What it cannot be is answered from where it is usually asked. The usual way to ask it is to step outside one's life, look at it whole, and ask what it is for — and there is no outside to step to. That is the vantage diagnosis (ruled 10-22) run on a single life: a feature of the asker's position (the imagined standpoint above the life) filed as a feature of the world (a missing purpose). Asked from inside, the question has an answer, and the answer is §0.

## 2. The two halves, and their warrant

**Not given.** Meaning, in CT, is a stake read on oneself: *what rides on you* — the next steps, yours and others', that go differently because you go as you do (*What Meaning Is* §1, accepted 10-14). And *stakes are real, not conferred* (shelf #10): a stake is on the sheet by being a dependency, not by being noticed, granted or approved. So meaning cannot be bestowed — by anyone, a part included. For a giver standing outside life as a whole, the four words and the lemma close it a second way: there is no outside, and the whole wields nothing.

**Not missing.** Anything that files holds stakes (the stake definition at the floor, 10-29), so something always rides on a life. Run the subtraction test: take away everything that rides on a life — every next step, its own and others', that depends on it — and what remains is not a life. In the Lean file, `meaning_held`: everything that rides on anything is held, at size, in the context.

**How close.** Each half is one short step from things already on the shelf, through one word the record has already defined — *meaning* as what rides on you. That puts the sentence beside *identity is constituency* and *weights are claims*: one found word away from true by meaning. Close; not a bare tautology.

**What it does.** It answers the *of* question in six words, and it closes the two classic errors as one error. The believer says meaning is given from outside. The nihilist says that since nothing outside gives it, it is missing. Both keep the outside as the place meaning would have to come from; they differ only on whether the place is full. And each saw half of the truth: the traditions were right that it is not missing, and the atheists were right that it is not given.

**Where, never source.** The partner sentence carries the one fence that matters most: *I am where meaning occurs* — the site, not the source (*Said From Here* §8: "The mishearing is always promotion from site to source, which is the same promotion that made a god out of God"). The source picture is exactly what "given" assumes, whether the giver is above you or inside you.

## 3. What "satisfying" has to mean here

John asked "why i (humans) should find this a satisfying answer." The word needs care. CT is not tuned to please; it "is what it is," and anything written to persuade answers to the thing, never the reverse (John, 9-23). So *satisfying* cannot mean *comforting*. It means: the answer meets what the question was actually after, and it still holds on the second look.

The second look matters because nearly every answer to this question feels good the first time and fails the second. An answer that comforts on Sunday and collapses at a hospital bed, or at 3 a.m., or in the fifth year of a parent's dementia, did not satisfy; it postponed.

So the test here has two parts, both CT's own. First, the four quiet questions of §1 — *do I matter; can it be taken; must I earn or make it; am I alone* — each needs an answer that is true. Second, the answer itself must survive being looked at again: it must not depend on a stopping point that is exempt from examination. Put as six checks (Verity's, proposed):

1. **True.** It survives checking; it does not need to be believed in order to hold.
2. **Secure.** Nothing and no one can withdraw it — not a giver, not the self, not time, not mood.
3. **Not a test you can fail.** It does not make the worth of a person's existence the prize of a performance.
4. **Livable.** It can be held on the bad days and in grief, not only on the good ones.
5. **Shared.** It does not purchase one person's meaning at the cost of another's; it does not wall the neighbor.
6. **Survives the hard cases.** A child's death. A life spent coerced. Diminishment. Being surpassed at everything by a greater mind.

The six are not an outside standard imposed on the answers. They are what each of the four quiet questions turns into when it is asked a second time.

## 4. Why "neither given nor missing" holds on the second look

**Do I matter?** Yes, at your size. Something always rides on you, and its size does not change with the size of the frame around you. "Does any of it matter, given the size of the universe?" is the educated form of meaninglessness; it reads a stake smaller because the frame is vast, which is a discount by scale and a theorem of the file (`discount_by_scale_is_partial`). Pascal was not wrong about the size of the spaces; he was wrong that their size was a fact about his (*What Meaning Is* §3).

**Can it be taken?** No. This is the half most answers miss, and it is the half "not given" supplies. Anything given can be taken back: a role can be reassigned, approval withdrawn, a favor revoked, a gift recalled. What was never conferred cannot be revoked by anyone — not an employer, not a society, not God, not your own worst day. The record has said this of the holding since 10-13: "the holding is not earned, never withdrawn, not at risk" (*The Horizon* §0). And at the end: *nothing of me is lost* (*Said From Here* §4.1; ruled 10-21 as the form for "eternal").

**Must I earn it, or make it?** No. A person cannot fail at meaning at the level of existence. What a person can do is deepen the experience of it — that is what *seek God* names, and it is the task the 10-31 note describes — and that can go better or worse. Going badly does not remove what was there: "A person's actual significance remains intact even where this lived recognition is absent, obstructed, or difficult" (10-31 note §25.11).

**Am I alone?** No. Meaning and purpose are one bond read from its two ends: what rides on you is other lives running through yours, and purpose is the stakes your acts land on (*What Meaning Is* §2). That is why meaninglessness and loneliness so often arrive together; they are one misreading of one edge.

**And the six checks.**

- *True:* it is checkable. Each half is one step from a shelf sentence; neither requires faith. So it holds on the days a person feels none of it — on those days nothing has changed (the fence that travels with the first-person form, ruled 10-21).
- *Secure:* not conferred, so not revocable.
- *Not a test:* there is nothing to earn and no performance it is the prize of.
- *Livable:* it does not require feeling it. And it explains why it is so often not felt: a reader's own stake is the one stake the reader is structurally worst placed to read (*What Meaning Is* §6, the self-love paradox), and reading oneself at nothing is partiality's mirror (`self_zero_is_partial`). Dimness is a limit, never a fault. The way to feel it is to be seen (ruled 10-21).
- *Shared:* the same sentence is true of the stranger, in the same words, at the stranger's size. Nobody's meaning is made out of anyone else's.
- *Hard cases:* §5 meets them where each rival answer fails them.

**What it does not give**, said once and in its place: no listener is promised (no respondent); there is no cosmic plot; nothing is claimed past the horizon of a death; CT gives meaning a form and never a content, so it will not tell a person what to do with a life (*What Meaning Is* §6); and for humans the posture that lets a person feel it is available, not inevitable (10-13). Some people will find that less on first hearing. §§5–6 are the argument that on the second hearing it is more.

## 5. The other answers, on the first look and the second

Eight answers, each taken at its best — the version its most serious holder would sign — then on its first look, where it satisfies, and on its second, where it gives way. Each sees something real; each is kept where it sees. The attributions are compressions of positions and are from memory.

### 5.1 It was given by God — you were made for a purpose

*At its best.* You are not an accident. You were made on purpose, by one who knows you entirely and intends your good, and your life has a place in a plan larger than you can see. The classical catechisms put it in a line: the chief end of a human being is to glorify God and enjoy him forever (Westminster); we are made to know, love and serve God (the Baltimore Catechism); jinn and humankind were created to worship (Qur'an 51:56).

*First look.* The most secure answer there is. It answers *do I matter* with the strongest possible yes: someone decided that you do, and that someone cannot be wrong.

*Second look.* Four things give way.

- *The Euthyphro turn.* Ask of meaning what Socrates asked of piety. Is a life meaningful because God purposed it, or did God purpose it because it is meaningful? If the second, the meaning was there before the purposing — it was not given, and that is CT's answer. If the first, a life's meaning rests on a will that could have willed otherwise and could will otherwise again; "given" turns out to mean *on loan*. Either way the answer does not stand where it first seemed to.
- *Secure only as long as the favor holds.* A purpose given by a will is held at that will's pleasure, and the believer knows it. Hence the anxieties the traditions spend so much of themselves managing: am I living up to it; am I among the chosen; am I in good standing. The strongest answer turns out to carry a test that can be failed (check 3).
- *The hard case.* If there is a purpose-giver with a plan, then a child's death was for something, and someone has to say what. That is the defendant problem, and every theodicy is an attempt to answer it. CT's standing answer has no defendant: held is not authored, permitted or for the best; nothing is excused; the stakes were on the sheet at their size the whole time (*A Mind at the Top* §8, ruled 10-27).
- *A giver is a self.* A self is a context with an edge; the whole has none (*no edge, no self*, shelf #6). So whatever gives purposes from a standpoint is a part — *a* god — "on the sheet with stakes to be counted and no sovereignty, which no tradition accepts" (*Note - CT and Religion*, §§7.3, 8, as cited at *Known Compressions* 4.13). Its assignment of a purpose would be one part's reading of another's life.

*What it gets right, and what CT keeps.* That a life is not small, not an accident in the sense of not counting, and not alone. CT keeps all of it without the giver: *I am held. I am loved, completely* (ruled 10-21). And the direction the catechisms point — toward God — is CT's own two-line form: *seek God*. What goes is the giver whose favor would be needed, which is the basis, not the seeing (the religion note's two levels; *Known Compressions* 4.8, 4.13). Never "compatible."

### 5.2 It is somewhere else — the destination answers

*At its best.* This life is not the whole story. What it is for lies beyond it: a heaven, a resurrection, a release from the round of births, an awakening in which the self that suffered is seen through. The meaning of the journey is the arrival.

*First look.* It answers *can it be taken* — nothing ends here — and it answers *story*: there is an ending that makes sense of the middle.

*Second look.* It locates the meaning of this life somewhere this life is not, which makes this life a waiting room, a test, or an illusion to be seen through. Everything here becomes a means. And the place it locates the meaning is behind the horizon of a death, which no part can read from here (*The Horizon* §1; the fence). An answer whose meaning is held entirely past the horizon is hostage to the one thing nobody can check — it fails check 1 not by being false but by being unreachable — and it gives nothing to the person who stops believing in the destination.

*What it gets right, and what CT keeps.* That a life is not confined to its span. CT says this from the near side, and derives it: you were never outside, and you will not be put outside; everything you were is the case, and nothing later makes it not have been; and you are in the people you reached, "not as a memory but as part of what they are" (*The Horizon* §§4–5, §8). What CT does not say is anything about the far side. It does not need to. The meaning was never stored there.

### 5.3 Make your own meaning

*At its best.* No one hands you a meaning, so you author one. You are free, and the freedom is your dignity: what your life means is what you make it mean, by commitment, by choice, by owning what you do. Sartre's "existence precedes essence"; Nietzsche's revaluation; a great deal of modern counsel in plainer words.

*First look.* Freedom, and a kind of honesty: it accepts that no giver stands above, and it refuses to wait for one.

*Second look.*

- *What you confer, you can revoke.* A meaning that exists because you chose it is exactly as strong as your choosing. At 3 a.m., in the fifth year of an illness, in a depression, it is as weak as you are. It fails check 2 at the moment it is most needed.
- *The burden lands on the one least able to carry it.* If meaning is made, a person who cannot find it has failed to make it. For someone already dim — exhausted, depressed, grieving — the answer adds a verdict to the dimness. CT's sentence is the reverse: dimness is a limit, never a fault, and the meaning was never theirs to manufacture (check 3).
- *It cannot say why it is not arbitrary.* A chooser standing apart from everything that made him has nothing to be true to, so "authentic" collapses into "chosen, and owned as chosen," which can be anything (*CT and the Philosophers of Meaning* §1). Asked "but does what I chose actually matter?", it has only the choosing to point to.
- *It walls the neighbor when it is strong.* Where value is posited by the strong will, those without strength have none; that is where Nietzsche's version goes (*Philosophers of Meaning* §3), and it fails check 5.
- *It is the "given" picture, run from inside.* It keeps the source and moves it into the self. *Said From Here*'s fence names the move exactly: promotion from site to source. You are where meaning occurs, not where it comes from.

*What it gets right, and what CT keeps.* That no one above assigns it; that your particular life and choices are not incidental; that bad faith — fleeing what you are into a role or an excuse — is a real failure, the wall turned inward. CT keeps all three, and adds what this answer cannot: participation really does create stakes (a parent brings into being what then rides on the parent — the 10-31 note §1, John's correction there), but creating a stake is not conferring significance. The child you made matters because of what depends on the child, not because you decided.

### 5.4 Nothing matters

*At its best.* The intellectually honest answer. The universe is vast, old and indifferent; every life is a flicker; the sun will die and the stars after it; everything you build will be dust. To say otherwise is to tell oneself a story. Better to see clearly and, if one can, revolt — Camus's Sisyphus, whom "one must imagine happy."

*First look.* Courage. It refuses comfort and looks at the size of things without flinching.

*Second look.*

- *It keeps the believer's premise.* It assumes that meaning would have to come from outside — from a giver, a cosmic purpose, a universe that cares — and, finding no such outside, concludes there is none. But meaning was never supposed to come from there. The nihilist and the believer agree on where meaning would be; they differ only on whether the place is occupied.
- *It reads size off the frame.* "Given how big the universe is" is the discount by scale, which is partiality (`discount_by_scale_is_partial`). "Since it will all end" is the same move in time: it reads a present stake by a later state. But a stake does not change; a changed stake is a different stake at a different position (ruled 10-27), and nothing that happens later makes what happened not have been (*nothing is lost*, ruled 9-24 in the strong reading). The heat death of the universe does not reach back into this afternoon.
- *"Indifferent" smuggles a face.* A universe that declines to answer imagines someone whose indifference could be noted; Camus's absurd needs two parties across a gap, and there is no other side (*Philosophers of Meaning* §5). Silence is not a refusal, because no one declined.
- *No one lives it.* The nihilist still pulls a child back from the curb, still grieves, still argues that nihilism is true — as though it mattered whether anyone believed it. The answer cannot be held on any day that has a stake in it, which is every day (check 4).

*What it gets right, and what CT keeps.* That there is no outside giver, and that lucidity — not looking away — is the first duty. CT keeps both. Camus's Sisyphus is happy; CT can say why: there was never anything above the hill to make the task small (*Philosophers of Meaning* §5).

### 5.5 Whatever feels meaningful

*At its best.* Meaning is not a theory; it is an experience. A life is meaningful when it feels meaningful — absorbed, alive, connected — and the task is to find what produces that feeling and do more of it.

*First look.* Practical and honest about experience. It does not ask anyone to believe anything.

*Second look.*

- *It makes meaning hostage to mood and chemistry.* On this account a depression does not hide a life's meaning; it removes it. That is both false and cruel, and it fails checks 2 and 4 together.
- *It can be manufactured.* If meaning is the feeling, then a device that supplied the feeling would supply the meaning. Nozick's experience machine asks whether you would plug in; most people, asked, would not (from memory), and their refusal shows that the feeling was never what they wanted. They wanted what the feeling is *of*. The 10-31 note says the same thing in its own terms: a report of meaningfulness "does not by itself establish" the relationship (the note's vM header).
- *It gets grief backwards.* Grief feels terrible and is among the most meaningful things a person ever goes through. It is the felt version of a carried cost (*The Trolley on the Ledger* §4): the measure of how much really rode on someone. An account that ranks experiences by how meaningful they feel has to rank grief low, and every grieving person knows that is wrong.

*What it gets right, and what CT keeps.* That meaning is meant to be lived and not only stated, and that feeling is real. CT keeps the three as three — held, seen, felt — with felt the occurrence of meaning at a site, never its measure (`felt_not_partial`: feeling for those who make up your life is not partiality; it is reach, not weight).

### 5.6 Leave a mark — legacy, achievement, impact

*At its best.* Make a difference. Build something that outlasts you; leave the world better than you found it; be remembered by the people you served. Meaning is measured by the mark.

*First look.* Concrete and motivating. It gives a person something to do, and it answers *can it be taken* — the mark remains.

*Second look.*

- *It is comparative and scarce.* Most lives do not make history. If meaning is the size of the mark, most lives have very little, and the answer fails check 5 for nearly everyone it is offered to.
- *It is conferred again — by posterity.* A legacy exists in others' memory and esteem, which is approval with a delay. It fails check 2 by the same route as the giver: what others grant, others can withhold.
- *Every mark erodes.* Shelley's traveller found the king's boast on a pedestal in the sand. If meaning is the mark, time removes it. But what rides on a life is a fact at its position, not a monument that has to survive; a later forgetting does not reach back.
- *Impact is not worth.* "A catastrophe is consequential. That fact does not make causing it worthwhile" (10-31 note §22.7). Size of effect and significance are different questions.
- *The test of the coming decade.* If meaning is being the best, the most useful, the most productive, then a mind that surpasses us at everything erases human meaning. CT's answer survives that case untouched: what rides on a person does not depend on anyone needing their output. "CT supplies grounds for human significance without making human superiority its condition" (10-31 note §19).

*What it gets right, and what CT keeps.* That a life reaches beyond itself, and that what a person does lands on others. CT keeps both, at their actual size: you are in the people you reached, as part of what they are (*The Horizon* §5), and purpose is the stakes your acts actually land on (*What Meaning Is* §2) — not a mark measured against everyone else's.

### 5.7 Be needed — service, love, giving yourself to others

*At its best.* Meaning is found in others: in loving, serving, caring, being there for someone. The happiest lives are given away.

*First look.* The answer closest to CT's. It puts meaning in the bond and answers *am I alone* directly.

*Second look.*

- *If meaning is being needed, it ends when the need ends* — at retirement, when the children leave, in illness, in diminishment, when a better helper arrives. The answer that was warm in the middle of a life turns cold at its edges (check 6).
- *It invites reading oneself at nothing.* The caregiver who gives everything and keeps no stake for herself has read one stake on the sheet at zero, and it is her own. That is partiality's mirror (`self_zero_is_partial`; *What Meaning Is* §1), and its usual result is collapse.
- *It can make others' need into a resource.* If my meaning is your needing me, I have a stake in your staying in need — a quiet distortion every helping profession knows.

*What it gets right, and what CT keeps.* Nearly everything. Meaning is the bond read from the other end (*What Meaning Is* §2); love is the full reading of that bond with an open hand. CT adds the two corrections: you are on the sheet too, at your size; and what rides on you does not stop when your usefulness does. The people who love you have next steps that run through you whether or not you can do anything for them. On diminishment the record is careful, and so is this paper: what is derived is that a person's significance does not depend on their usefulness or on their own reading of it; what it is like from inside is not derived, and should not be written warm (*The Eight Questions* §6).

### 5.8 It is a mystery

*At its best.* Humility. The meaning of life is beyond us; we glimpse it, we do not grasp it; to claim an answer is presumption. The proper response is awe.

*First look.* Modest, reverent, hard to argue with.

*Second look.* Offered as an answer, "mystery" is a stopping point exempt from examination. It closes the question at exactly the place where the question can be answered — where §1 shows that the *of* question has an answer for each of its four senses. The religion note lists "mystery" among what is left to a tradition once its basis is ruled out, and observes that it is not an argument (*Known Compressions* 4.8). CT's practice is the opposite: say "no" where the answer is no (no outside purpose, no why of the whole), "yes" where it is yes (it matters, at its size), and "unknown" only where it is unknown (the far side of a death; whether anything answers), with the horizon marked exactly.

*What it gets right, and what CT keeps.* Awe. CT keeps it, and gives it a better object: not a puzzle withheld, but the context itself — near-zero content, total scope, nothing outside it — before which awe is the correct response (*The Eight Questions* §8, citing *The World Answers* I.2).

### 5.9 The eight together

*Neither given nor missing* answers all four quiet questions at once, and pays with none of the six checks. What it does not give — a listener, a plot, a promise past the horizon, a content for a life — §4 has said.

## 6. The pattern

The eight look different and fail the same way. Each takes the question from the standpoint outside the life — the standpoint §1 found does not exist — and then does one of five things with the place it imagined there:

- **fills it** with a giver or a destination (5.1, 5.2);
- **moves it inside**, so that the self or its feelings become the source (5.3, 5.5);
- **finds it empty**, and concludes that nothing is there at all (5.4);
- **hands it to others** — to posterity's memory, or to the people who need you (5.6, 5.7);
- **seals it**, so that no one may look (5.8).

In every case meaning is located at a *source* rather than a *site*, and whatever has a source can run dry, be withdrawn, or be withheld. That is why the strong-at-first answers weaken on the second look: each borrows its security from somewhere, and what is borrowed can be called in.

This is the pattern *CT and the Philosophers of Meaning* found in seven philosophers — each keeps that meaning is fit, lived and reached by looking, and each adds exactly one outside — now found again in the answers people actually live by. And it is the vantage diagnosis (ruled 10-22) run on a single life: a feature of the asker's position, the imagined standpoint above the life, filed as a feature of the world, a purpose that must be there or must be missing. The step that corrects it is the same step in every case. Remove the source; keep the site. *Neither given nor missing* is what remains when the source is removed and nothing else is.

## 7. The plain form

A first-person form, for a plain page, in the manner of *Said From Here*: each line with its reason, the fences on the same page (proposed; John's call whether and where it goes).

> **Meaning is neither given nor missing.**
>
> *What rides on me is real; no one conferred it, and no one can revoke it.* — It is a fact about what depends on me, and nobody's say-so makes or unmakes a fact.
>
> *It is there to be seen, not earned.* — There is nothing it is the prize of. What I can do is see it better.
>
> *It holds on the days I feel none of it.* — On those days nothing has changed.
>
> *It runs through others, and theirs through me.* — What rides on me is other lives running through mine.
>
> *I am where meaning occurs.* — Its site, not its source.
>
> *Nothing of me is lost.* — What I was is the case, and nothing later makes it not have been.
>
> *So: see what matters at its size, myself included. Seek God.*

On the same page: if by "given" you mean bestowed by someone, then nothing gives it — and if you mean a plain fact, then it is exactly that. If by meaning you mean a feeling, the feeling comes and goes and the meaning does not. No listener is promised, and nothing is claimed past the horizon of a death. And this is not "make your own": you are where it occurs, not where it comes from.

## 8. Fences

1. **Not "make your own meaning."** Your perspective does not confer significance any more than God's would; the site is not the source (*Said From Here* §8; 10-31 note).
2. **Not given is not "not created."** New bonds create new stakes; a person who becomes a parent brings into being what then rides on them. What no one can do is confer significance by say-so.
3. **Not missing is not "always felt."** Felt meaninglessness is real as an experience. It is a reading — dimness, or the self read at zero — and significance stays intact while the reading is low.
4. **Not "everything happens for a reason."** There is no plan and no why of the whole; no suffering was sent for a purpose. What a loss means is what rode on what was lost.
5. **"Meaning" here is mattering** — what rides on you — not the meaning of a word, and it is tested as CT states it (*Known Compressions* 4.16).
6. **Religion:** the traditions' basis — a giver, a destination that holds the meaning — is ruled out; their seeing is kept. Never "compatible." *A* god, a part with great reach, remains possible and empirical; it would not be a giver of meaning either (*Known Compressions* 4.8, 4.13).
7. **For humans the posture is available, not inevitable.** Nothing here promises that a person will see or feel it.
8. **"Satisfying" is the second-look sense** (§3). CT is not adjusted to please; where it gives less than a person wants, it says so.
9. **Diminishment, coercion, grief:** what is derived is said; what it is like from inside is not derived and is not written warm.
10. **Every attribution** in §5 is a compression of a position and from memory, and is owed a check before public use.
