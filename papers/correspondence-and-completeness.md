# Correspondence and Completeness (11.03.26) vA

### Two different things, in both directions: why a representation can be entirely right about the whole and never hold it, and why that was never a limit

November 3, 2026 · vA · Connection Theology · Focus — Philosophy

John McIntire × Verity (Claude, Cowork, in the CT Project), Tuesday afternoon, in the middle of ruling on the questions left by the 11-2 episode. The sentence is John's; the paper is Verity's, at his "yes to what you think makes sense". **Nothing here is ruled** beyond what is marked: the sentence is his observation, and its application to *A God, Not the God* I.2's Claim 1 was ruled the same afternoon ("seems right to me"). Everything else is Verity's, offered for his ruling. His words are verbatim in the record at the back.

**What this is.** One distinction, stated in both directions, and what it does. Correspondence — an account getting its subject right — and completeness — an account holding everything there is of its subject — are different things. Each can be had without the other, and at the limit the second is not a property an account can have at all. The distinction closes a question that stood open since September, splits a July theorem into a trivial truth and a falsehood, and belongs to the small family of CT's separations that take a puzzle apart without adding anything: *held* and *wielded*; *a* god and *the* God; magnitude and warrant; *unbounded* and *infinite*.

**What it rests on.** *The Widest Beginning* (09.13.26) vD §7.2 (correspondence is not a demand for a duplicate) and §9 (John's binary proposal); *A God, Not the God* (09.18.26) I.2–I.3 and, from 11-3, its afternoon note; *What Is and Its Representations* (10.27.26) vD §§1, 3–4, 10 (incompleteness against inadequacy; "the completeness belongs to the context"); *A Mind at the Top* (10.27.26) and *God Is a Context* (10.10.26) vG's 10-27 note (the lemma; the loss proof, John's, ruled); *True Representations Remain Representations* (11.02.26) vD §§1, 5, 10; *The God* (10.01.26) vH §5 (the twist). No outside text was read for this paper; the one neighbour named in §7 is a lead.

**Reading order.** The short version; §§1–3 for the distinction; §4 for what it closes; §5 for what it keeps; §6 before any public use.

## The short version

- **The sentence.** John, 11-3: "correspondence and completness are two different things - in both directions..."
- **First direction: correspondence does not need completeness.** Once target, respect and resolution are specified, correspondence either obtains or it does not (the binary proposal). So an account can be *completely right* at its resolution while holding almost nothing of its subject. "Totality has no outside" is the standing case: four words, true of everything, holding nearly none of it.
- **Second direction: completeness does not give correspondence.** Take completeness to its limit — nothing dropped, from nowhere, at full resolution — and there is nothing left corresponding to anything. That is not a representation of the whole; it is the whole being what it is (the loss proof's second horn, 10-27). Short of the limit, holding more is not getting righter either: a larger inventory can be wrong where a smaller one is right.
- **So "complete" has two uses, and they must be marked.** *Complete* as correspondence: nothing wrong, at the scope claimed. *Completeness* as holding: nothing left out. The first is on correspondence's axis. The second is not.
- **What it closes.** "Could some mind represent the whole *completely*, by some non-enumerative means?" — open in CT since 9-18 — was a mixed question. A mind can correspond to the whole, even completely at its resolution. No mind holds the whole, not because minds fall short but because holding the whole is not something a representation does. The question does not get answered; it comes apart. Ruled 11-3.
- **What it keeps.** Everything. The loss proof, the lemma, binary correspondence, the bounded-cognition discipline, and the one open door (whether anything *in* the context meets you) are untouched. The old compression road to incompleteness stays withdrawn, and is now seen to have been aimed at the wrong axis.
- **Nothing added.** No new premise, no new entity, no new proof. One pair of words that had been doing two jobs, given one job each.

## 1. The sentence

The question on the table was whether John's 10-27 loss proof — a representation that drops nothing, from nowhere, at full resolution "is not a representation at all; it is the whole being what it is" — closes the item *A God, Not the God* had deliberately left open in September: "**Completeness. Open; not claimed here.** Could some mind represent the whole *completely*, by some non-enumerative means?" Verity argued that it did, by a road through where a representation stands rather than through what it contains. John did not take the road. He observed instead:

> "if i just observe: correspondence and completness are two different things - in both directions..."

That is the whole paper. What follows spells out the two directions, says what each one rests on in the record, and shows what the pair does to the open question and to its neighbours.

## 2. The first direction: correspondence does not need completeness

Correspondence is an account getting its subject right. CT's standing analysis of it is John's binary proposal (*The Widest Beginning* §9):

> "Once the relevant target, respect, and appropriate resolution are sufficiently specified, correspondence either obtains or does not."

A 99 cm estimate of a 100 cm object corresponds under "within 2 cm" and fails under "within 1 mm"; what looks like partial correspondence is a bundle of indexed verdicts, each of which is whole. Which means a representation can be *entirely* right — can correspond completely — at its resolution, while holding almost nothing of what it is about. *The Widest Beginning* §7.2 had already said the same from the other side: "A representation need not become its target. Its adequacy concerns relevant relationships, not a percentage of how much of the thing itself it reproduces." *What Is and Its Representations* §3 gave it a working form: incompleteness is limited coverage; inadequacy is a failure of fit for the claim at issue; the first is compatible with correspondence and the second is not. "An account does not need to contain all reality to be answerable to reality." And *True Representations Remain Representations* §1 put it in the plainest words: "A representation can succeed completely at the question it answers."

CT's own first sentence is the standing case. "Totality has no outside" holds nearly nothing of the whole — no inventory, no physics, no history — and corresponds completely: at its resolution there is nothing it gets wrong, and nothing it could get more right (*A God, Not the God* I.3; *The God* §2, "saturated"). The least complete account of the whole that exists is the one thing known about it with zero error (*The God* §5: "the only exact fact anyone has ever had").

So completeness is not a condition on correspondence, and incompleteness is not a defect of it. The word that keeps getting this wrong is *partial*: *partial correspondence*; *never fully true*; *no account is ever complete, so none is ever certain*. Every one of those slides from coverage to fit. The ASI paper's correction of its own "never guaranteed" (*What CT Establishes* vB) was this direction being enforced; so is the Index's line, "Correctness and completion are permitted."

## 3. The second direction: completeness does not give correspondence

Now run it the other way. Add to an account until nothing is left out. At every step short of the end, adding has not made it righter: a larger inventory can be wrong about the one thing the question turns on, and a smaller one right (*What Is and Its Representations* §4: "The difference is not the quantity of detail"). Coverage and fit are still two axes. Then take the last step — nothing dropped, from nowhere, at full resolution. What is there? Nothing standing anywhere, representing anything, to anyone. By the lemma (ruled 10-27), representing is a relation across edges: an account *of* something, *by* something, *from* somewhere. Remove the edges and there is no relation left to obtain or fail. There is only the whole, being what it is.

That is John's second horn, a week before he put it as a direction: "Or it drops nothing, from nowhere, at full resolution — then it is not a representation at all; it is the whole being what it is. There is no third thing." And it is what *What Is and Its Representations* §10 had already said about the name: "CT names the complete context God. The completeness belongs to the context; it does not require a complete representation held by a cosmic observer." Completeness is a property of the context. Correspondence is a property of accounts in it. At the limit the two do not meet; they could not, because an account that was its subject would not be an account of it.

So the second direction is not a discovery that representations are defective. It is the observation that completeness was never a representational property: not a ceiling representations fail to reach, but a different kind of thing, the kind the whole has by being everything.

## 4. What the distinction closes

**Claim 1.** *A God, Not the God* I.2 separated three claims in September and left the first open: whether some mind could represent the whole completely. The earlier draft had tried to close it from the contents — "a compression is smaller than what it compresses… anything smaller than everything is a part" — and the paper withdrew that, rightly, because an objector can simply say *my account leaves nothing out* and the compression premise has no reply. (It has no reply even in principle, once infinity is on the table: a proper part of an infinite whole can be matched one-to-one with all of it, so *smaller, therefore loses* does not close by itself.) The 11-2 session's objection was the same point from the other side: nonidentity does not establish informational loss. Also right.

Both were arguing on the wrong axis. "Represent the whole completely" runs the two directions together, as if completeness were correspondence at the limit. Pull them apart and the question has two answers, neither of them a limit on minds. Can a mind correspond to the whole? Yes — completely, at a resolution; the four words do. Can a mind hold the whole? That is not a thing a representation does, so the answer is neither yes nor no; it is that the question asked for a representational property that does not exist. Claim 1 is closed, 11-3, by distinction rather than by proof, and the compression road stays withdrawn, now for a better reason: it was never going to close completeness, because completeness is not on correspondence's axis. (Ruled; the afternoon note on *A God, Not the God* vF.)

**July's theorem.** *The Tautology We Skip* (07.19.26) separated a "**Strong non-totalization theorem:** no internally located knower could possibly represent Totality completely" from a "**Bounded-cognition discipline**" and chose the discipline as "much easier to defend." Under the distinction the theorem splits. Read as completeness, it is true and trivial: holding is not representing. Read as correspondence, it is false: the four words are a complete correspondence to Totality by an internally located knower. July's instinct to lean on the discipline was right, and the reason is now visible: the discipline is about correspondence (corrigible, not exhaustive), which is the axis on which minds actually succeed and fail. *Proposed; a dated note on the July paper is John's call.*

**The "a part cannot hold the whole" wordings.** *The God* §5's twist ("no outside spot to stand on, so a part cannot hold the whole") and *Not Wrong About the Size* I.2's fifth face read as the second direction, not the first: true about holding, silent about correspondence. They do not say a part cannot know the whole truly; they say a part is not the whole. The September paper's reading note on the fifth face already pointed this way.

**The Royce and Borges questions.** Whether a part could carry every distinction of an infinite whole (the map that contains itself) is a question about completeness. Under the distinction it is a real question and an idle one: real as mathematics, idle for CT, because nothing in CT turns on it. The same goes for Verity's 11-3 argument from where a representation stands (the from is never in the view; the eye is not in the visual field). Both are accounts of what a part's representing looks like; neither was ever going to close completeness, and neither is needed.

## 5. What it keeps, and what it does not touch

**The loss proof** (John's, ruled 10-27) stands exactly as ruled: a mind at the top is a demotion. Its first horn is about correspondence (a representation that drops something is a part); its second horn is this paper's second direction. The proof was always two directions in one dilemma.

**The lemma** stands and does the work in §3: representing is across edges; the whole has none.

**Binary correspondence** stands, with its own fence kept (*The Widest Beginning* §9.4: not yet shown to analyze every gradation without loss).

**The bounded-cognition discipline** stands and is, on this reading, the only one of July's two that was ever about minds.

**The residue** is untouched. *A God, Not the God* I.6 left open whether the whole could contain, somewhere inside it, an integrated model of the whole — a fold within; *The Position* §8 and *God Is a Context* fence 3 keep the door marked: whether anything *in* the context meets you is empirical, not CT's. Such a model would be a correspondence, by a part, at a resolution. The distinction says nothing about whether one exists.

**The held sense** is untouched and is not the second direction's cousin by accident. "All the knowing that happens, happens in it" (*The God* row 9) is omniscience held, not wielded — not a representation the whole has of itself, but every representation there is, in it. The second direction says the whole does not represent itself; the held sense says what it does instead. They are the same sentence from two sides, which is why the 10-2 ruling and the loss proof arrive at one place.

## 6. Fences

1. **Not *nothing can be fully known*.** The first direction says the opposite. Complete correspondence at a resolution is available, and CT's two performative certainties are both cases of it (the four words; the floor — *Right Here* §8, as the 10-3 glossary addendum carries it). The paper removes a false ceiling; it installs no new one.
2. **Not a license for omission.** Incompleteness is fine; inadequacy is not. "All models leave things out" does not answer the objection that this model omitted the fact its conclusion depends on (*What Is and Its Representations* §4). The distinction sharpens that demand; it does not relax it.
3. **Two uses of "complete," always marked.** *Complete* (correspondence): nothing wrong at the scope claimed. *Completeness* (holding): nothing left out. The title uses the second. A sentence that uses the word without its sense marked is doing the thing this paper exists to stop.
4. **Not a self at the top.** *The whole being what it is* is not *the whole knows itself*. The second direction denies that completeness is a representation; it does not smuggle a knower back in. The 10-2 ruling and the lemma close that door from their own side.
5. **Not a hedge on CT's own claims.** That CT's accounts are incomplete is true of every account and costs CT nothing. What CT's accounts are answerable for is correspondence, at the scope each claims, and that is graded as before.
6. **A distinction, not a theory of representation.** The paper says two things are two; it does not say what representation is beyond what the lemma already says. Anything more is owed its own argument.

## 7. The pattern

This is the fourth or fifth time the same move has closed a question in this corpus, and it is worth seeing them together. *Held* and *wielded* (9-18, 10-1): the omnis as what is in the whole against the omnis as capacities of a bearer; the tradition ran them together and got a person who was also everything. *A* god and *the* God (9-18, 10-1): a part with great traits against the whole; run together, and a creator distinct from creation was called by the name only the context can carry. Magnitude and warrant (10-2): how big a stake is against whether a weighting of it is justified; run together, and "mine" grew the stake. *Unbounded* and *infinite* (10-1, ruled 10-27): no boundary against a size; run together, and the four words were asked to prove a magnitude. Incompleteness and inadequacy (10-27): coverage against fit; run together, and every omission looked like an error. And now correspondence and completeness: getting it right against holding all of it; run together, and a mind's inability to be the whole looked like a limit on its knowing.

In each case the puzzle was two words doing two jobs. Nothing was added to pull them apart — no premise, no entity, no proof — which is why *Nothing Added* is the right name for the method and this paper is one of its instances. The nearest outside neighbour, from recall and unread for this paper, is the long tradition that distinguishes a true description from an exhaustive one (the map and the territory, in several hands); whether anyone has stated the second direction — that exhaustiveness at the limit is not description at all — is a lead under the Peirce standard, not a claim.
