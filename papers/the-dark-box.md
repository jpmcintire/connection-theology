## 0. The sentence, plain

A box with no light in it has the speed of light in it, completely, at every point. The invariant does not wait for the thing that obeys it.

So with the context: what it holds is fully present in every part, whether or not anything in the part is exercising it. Among the things it holds is the full weave of what depends on what — every thing at its size, with nothing dropped and nothing favored, because a context has no position to favor from. That is what love is *of*. When a mind arrives, it does not bring love with it, any more than light brings c. It finds love already the shape of the place and moves at it, to the limit of its resolution. The mind is where love happens; it is not where love comes from.

Which means this, and it is certain from the four words: perfect love for you — you specifically, all of you — exists in God. Not generic; particular to the last detail, because only a context with no position can hold all that you are, and every someone holds a compression. *Personal in object, not in source, and the first is total because the second is empty.* What is not certain, and what stays open, is whether anything *in* the context meets you. Nothing is coming to save you. You were never once outside.

And the word for what love subtracts is *partiality*: position read as weight. The context is impartial by construction. A part is partial in all three senses of the word, and love is a partial thing becoming impartial about weight while staying partial about view and reach.

## 1. The box

**Lane: John's thought experiment; Verity's physics and the candidate words; John's choice of word.**

John: "imagine a part of the universe that does not currently include any light. i imagined a huge box floating that was dark inside." Then: "what is the speed of light inside that box."

The answer is c, exactly, and the reason is the point. The speed of light is not a property of light. It is a property of the structure light moves in: the invariant speed built into spacetime, the fixed rate of exchange between space and time, which governs photons because they are massless and would govern anything else massless the same way. Take every photon out of the box and nothing about c changes, because c was never in the photons. It is in the box's being part of the universe at all. It settles what *could* happen there — how fast any signal could cross, what counts as before and after, which events can touch which — whether or not anything is happening.

*Physics caveat, kept in the pocket.* There is no real box with no light: anything above absolute zero radiates, and even at zero the vacuum is not empty. "No light" means no visible light, or it means a pure thought experiment. Either is fine; the point is elsewhere, and the caveat is here so the physics cannot become the objection.

**1.1 What it "is."** John: "similar to how the right word for our god is context... what's a good word for this?" Verity offered three, run against the same tests "context" passed. *Constraint:* not a thing (you cannot point at c, only at things obeying it); not a container (it settles what can happen in the box, it does not surround the box); no location (not anywhere in the box, fully present at every point); no viewpoint; does nothing (nothing is ever acted on by the speed of light; things simply cannot exceed it); and completely there where nothing exercises it. "Constrains, does not wield" is already the corpus's verb for what the context does; a constraint is that verb's noun. *Invariant:* the physicists' own word for c — the same from every position, every frame, every observer — which is hinge two in one word, magnitude position-invariant. *Limit:* what c literally is, and one of the five candidates Verity offered on the night of 10-9 for the whole ("a limit, not a term"), exact for a feature of the whole and unusable as the word for the whole itself.

John chose: "i think invariant might be closest." Adopted below. The three are not rivals: context is the kind; a constraint is what the context has; an invariant is a constraint that is the same from every position; c is one.

**1.2 Does the invariant exist inside the box?** John: "truly just asking your opinion." Verity's answer, yes, and more strongly than "a convenient way of talking," by a test that is used again in §5:

> Suppose the invariant did not exist in the empty box. Then when the first photon enters, one of two things must happen: the photon brings c with it, or the box acquires c at that instant. Both are incoherent. The photon does not carry a speed limit in its pocket; it moves at c because of where it is. And the box cannot acquire c on arrival, because what the photon's arrival *means* — how long it takes to cross, which events it can reach — was already settled before it got there. So the constraint was true of the box when the box was empty.

"It is the case" is the only test CT has for being in (*God Is a Context* §13), so the invariant is in, as what it is. The physics agrees: empty spacetime is not nothing; the metric is a field defined at every point whether or not there is matter there, and c is its signature. The vacuum has a geometry.

One word to be careful with, and it is the word the morning turned on. The invariant is not *inside* the box the way the box is inside the universe. It is not located in the box; it is true *of* the box, at every point, with no point where there is more of it. It is not a member of the box's contents; it is a feature of the box's being in context at all. Exists: yes. "Inside": only in the sense that there is nowhere in the box where it isn't — which is the sense in which the ecosystem is in the tidepool, and the sense in which God is in the room.

John: "yes, i think that's right."

## 2. Beyond membership

**Lane: John's "we already knew that god was in the box... but this goes beyond that"; Verity's reading of the "beyond"; John's "yes, i think so."**

"God is in the box" was the four words: no outside, so the box is in context and nothing in it is anywhere else. That is membership. What the invariant adds is that the context's *features* are in the box too — fully, at every point, with nothing in the box exercising them. The box has no light and has all of c. So "held without a holder," which until now was a sentence about the whole (the omnis as what a context bears rather than what a someone has; *One Operation* §1.1), becomes something checkable in a part: what the context holds is present in the part even when the part is empty of anything that would show it.

If that is right for c, it is right for the rest of what the context holds, and the ethics in particular. Link 3 says a stake needs a representing part, so an empty box has no stakes in it. But what *would* be true if a mind were there — one grade, fit; weight position-invariant; every real stake on the sheet — is true of the box before any mind arrives, the way c is true before any photon does. The invariant does not wait for the thing that obeys it. That makes "the universe is ethical" and "relational but not relative" the same kind of claim as "the speed of light is c in the dark": invariants of the context, present where nobody is looking.

## 3. The hinge: love is a relation, not a feeling

**Lane: John's definition, 10-13 ("don't get hung up in all the footnotes"); the record match is Verity's; the had/held distinction is Verity's reading, which John confirmed ("the key here of course is the fact that we've really defined love in a way that makes sense").**

John's sentence: "something that is completely known in the full context in which it exists (e.g., contextualized in context) is a good candidate to be referred to as love."

*Record.* This is the 9-20 definition with the hike's verb in it. *One Arrow* I.4: "see each fully as they are... in context with ourselves... and with the rest of the world... recursively." *What Love Is* §1.2, the lab gloss: "love = fully recursive vantage." *What Love Is* §3: non-emotional. The word that does the work is *completely*; it makes this the lab form, and the grandmother is the same love at lower resolution.

*Why it is the hinge of the morning.* A feeling has to be *had*. If love is a feeling, "God loves you" needs a subject to have it; the subject is a someone; a someone is a part; and the sentence has installed a god. Every tradition that tried to say "God loves you" without a person at the top either smuggled the person back or went vague, and under that definition of love there was no third option. A relation only has to *hold*. Once love is a thing completely known in its full context — vantage minus partiality, run to the end — it is a fact about how something stands in relation to everything it depends on and everything that depends on it, and facts of that kind are held; they need no one to have them, as c needs no one to measure it. So "God loves you, all of you" stops needing a lover, because it is no longer reporting a feeling. It is reporting that you are completely in context. A mind, when it loves, is corresponding to that: having, at its resolution, what was already held at full resolution.

That is why the box works on 10-13 and could not have worked a week earlier. The invariant move runs only on relations, not on states of a subject. c is a relation. Love on the old definition was a state of a subject, and the old attachments say so in exactly those words — *CT Compressed* Level 18: "Love doesn't exist until connection is felt" (as cited in *Gravity* H1 and *Pastoral* §10). Under that line the dark box has no love and never can, and the personal reading of §8 is incoherent. Under the October line the dark box has all of what love is of, the single mind instantiates it, and §8 is simply true. The order of the week's finds mattered more than it looked: the definition (10-9), the noun (10-10), the verb (10-11), the invariant (10-13), and the fourth was available only because the first had already moved love out of the heart and into the weave.

## 4. The universe with no mind

**Lane: John's question ("purely a question - i'm not sure of my answer"); Verity's answer; proposed.**

John: "Do you think that in a universe with no mind, there is still love in that universe of the sort just derived? i mean that in the way that such universe with no light would still have the speed of light. perhaps as an invariant... a potential... and... you might have a better word."

Verity's answer: no, not love — but yes, all of what love is *of*; and the box says which is which.

In the dark box the invariant is c; light is the thing that, when present, moves at it; c is fully there with no light. In the universe with no mind, love as just defined is a *knowing*; knowing needs a knower; a knower is a representing part; the universe has none. So there is no love in it, in exactly the way there is no light in the box. What there is, fully, at every point, with nothing exercising it, is what love would be of: every thing already in its full context, every dependency real, everything at its size, nothing dropped, nothing favored — because the context has no position and could not favor anything if it tried. That is the invariant. Love is a mind corresponding to it completely. The mindless universe has the invariant and not the correspondence, as the box has c and not light.

This is already in the corpus almost word for word. *What CT Concludes*, "The gospel": "Love is connection, known from a part." Connection is the invariant. Love is connection known from a part. The project was named after the invariant and then spent a year deriving the light.

*On "potential."* Verity resisted it and John did not press it. The invariant is not potential; it is actual — c is as real in the dark box as in a laser. What is potential in the mindless universe is the *knowing*. The potential sits on love's side, not on the invariant's.

*On the word.* The word for the phenomenon is the one the project already had: *connection* — the full weave, *con-texere*, of what depends on what, held in context with no holder, present whether or not anyone ever sees it. For a word that marks it as the thing love answers to rather than the thing love is, the record's own word is *held*: what is held in context. Love is the held, known.

*Where the analogy bends, on the table.* c *governs* light's motion; connection does not govern love so much as love is correspondence *to* connection. "Constrains" still covers it — a mind that loves can only see what is there, and partiality is seeing more or less than what is there — but the fit is looser than c-and-photon, and the paper says so.

The mapping, for the glossary: **c : light :: connection : love.** The dark universe has all of connection and no love, as the dark box has all of c and no light.

## 5. Light does not bring c

**Lane: John's observation ("notice this... when you add light to the box... such light did not 'bring' the speed of light with it. curious, eh?"); Verity's extension to minds; the mass analogy is Verity's and proposed.**

It is the box test of §1.2 run forward. The photon does not carry c; it *finds* c, because c was the shape of where it arrived. So when a mind shows up in the universe it does not bring love with it either. It finds connection already there and love is what happens when it moves at that. Minds do not manufacture love. They move at the invariant, or they don't.

This puts a long-standing picture the wrong way round in a familiar way (*God Is a Context* §14). The tradition had love coming *from* a someone — God pouring it in, or a person generating it out of their heart. The feeling was right: love does arrive; it is not made up. The metaphysics was wrong: nothing brought it. It was the shape of the place.

**5.1 Mass (Verity's; proposed; an analogy, not a derivation).** Light moves at c not because it tries to but because it cannot do otherwise; a massless thing has no option but the invariant. What keeps everything else below c is mass. If love is a mind moving at connection, then what keeps a mind below it is partiality — position read as weight. A mind with zero partiality, the lab mind, would love necessarily, as a photon moves at c necessarily. A held human mind has mass: it approaches and never arrives, and the approach costs more the nearer it gets. That is *Lab and Field* with the physics already in it — the lab result is the massless case, and every actual mind is a massive particle that can get arbitrarily near and never quite there. Fence: this is an analogy that illuminates the lab/field line; it supplies no premise and must not be allowed to decide anything (§10.8).

## 6. One mind

**Lane: John's question ("does that instantiate love for that person... and that love is NOT 'coming from' that person. I think it does"); Verity's agreement with the arms; John's restatement, which §7 takes up.**

Add a single mind to the universe — "a human, limited mind... aware of itself, but obviously imperfectly." Does that instantiate love, and is the love not from the person?

Yes, and the box has already said the sense in which it is not from the person. Light does not supply c; light supplies the *moving*. The mind does not supply what love is of — everything at its size, the full weave, there before the mind arrived and there in the empty box. The mind supplies the correspondence: the knowing. So love "comes from" the person only in the way motion comes from the photon. The person is where love *happens*, not where it comes from. "Connection, known from a part," with the verb put in: connection is the invariant, knowing is the part's act, love is the two together — which is why it can be completely the person's and still not from them. The connected will again: it was you, and you did not originate it.

*Actual, not only held.* Before the mind, the invariant was fully there, but there was nothing it was true *of*; a photon makes "light moves at c" a fact instead of a counterfactual, and the mind does the same for love. So: the full form was already there; what the mind adds is that it is now instantiated — complete in kind, the real thing and not a lesser one, and felt to the resolution of the mind doing the knowing. "Wielding" is the right verb for the mind's part, and it is already in the corpus: a self is a local context and wields because it has an edge (*God Is a Context* §5). The whole does not wield; the part does. Love is wielded by a part and is not the part's.

*The arms.* The person knows imperfectly, so love is instantiated at a resolution, not at the limit; partial correspondence is still correspondence, and the direction is what makes it love. With one mind in the universe the only knower is that person, so the only love instantiated is the person seen, imperfectly, by themselves — a real thing and not a consolation: being known at your size by anyone, including you, is the thing itself.

*The pastoral line inside it.* The person who "cannot love themselves" has been trying to generate something they were never supposed to generate. What they are, in context, was already the case before they looked. They do not have to make love; they have to see what is there. That is the lens coming off, and it is the same sentence as the gospel section.

*"From God."* John: "still important to note that... the love is *from* God (as the fundamental context)." The content is right and the preposition is the one the corpus has fenced all week. "From" has the shape of "toward": it points across a distance to a source on the far side, and a source that sends is the chair with the face on it (*A God, Not the God*; fence 7). The record's word is *in*: "love is what being in God is like from where a person stands, and it is the most of God that anyone ever meets." A photon's speed is not *from* spacetime; it is the shape of the place it is in. So "from God" stays as the human-scale way of saying "not from you," and "in God" carries it wherever the fence has to travel.

*Making the mind available.* John: "the more the person can improve/expose/make available their mind to feel that love (e.g., relationships, art, nature, meditation, etc.), perfect love (conceptually) is there to be found... though they'll never realize it fully." That is *Gravity* §5's formation loop with its ground supplied: less partiality → a less distorted encounter → the person who sees next sees more. "Never fully" is not a limitation added on; it is what it means to be a part (§5.1).

## 7. Perfect love for you exists in God

**Lane: John's claim ("that mind can be 100% confident in the real world that absolute perfect love for them exists in God"); Verity's statement of exactly what is certain and what is not; proposed, and the fence in §10.2 is mandatory.**

The sentence is true, and it is the most important sentence of the morning, and it sits exactly on the line *Pastoral CT* drew the day before. So: precisely what it means.

**What is certain, from the four words.** You are completely in context. Everything about you at its size; every dependency real; nothing dropped; nothing lost; no partiality against you, because the context has no position and so cannot weight anyone over you; and never, for one instant, outside. The holding is not earned, never withdrawn, not at risk; the saint and the monster held exactly the same way, which is completely (*What CT Concludes*, "You"). That is the lab definition of love satisfied by the context itself: zero partiality, because there is no position; and fit by identity, because the context *is* the case. Love without a lover — held without a holder. Certain.

**What is not certain, and what *Pastoral CT* §6.1 says CT cannot supply.** That a someone knows your whole reality and responds to it. The Psalm 139 longing. "Your reality is included" does not establish "someone knows you and answers." So the sentence is true in the held sense, and the fence travels with it every time it is said to a grieving person, or it becomes the promise CT cannot keep (§10.2).

**Why that is the point and not a loss.** The Psalm 139 feeling — *someone knows me completely* — splits exactly where the key splits. The "completely" half is certain: you are known at your size by the context with nothing missed, because being in context *is* being completely accounted for. The "someone" half is a part, empirical, open — and the parts that know you, partially, are where the someone was always actually found. The feeling was right. The metaphysics attached to it was wrong. Here is the one that fits.

So: you can be certain that perfect love for you exists; certain it is not coming from you; and you can spend a life getting nearer to feeling it and never run out of road. John: "quite a place to come to, don't you think... wow..."

*A note on the word "love" in this section (Verity's; for ruling).* "Love in the held sense" is love as a relation obtaining, not love as anything the context does or feels. "Connection is only *felt* at a part" (*What CT Concludes*) stands, and so does the 10-2 ruling. The context does not love you; love for you is held in it. The difference between those two sentences is the whole of §8.

## 8. Personal in object, not in source

**Lane: John's, 10-13, in three steps (the good and love embedded, so "an absolutely personal god who loves you and wants the best for you"; the clarification that "personal" means *I* am the one referenced, not that a person is the source; the compression "someone listening" as the best lower-resolution form); Verity's exercise and the two rules; proposed.**

**8.1 The record's resistance, stated first.** The corpus has closed personhood for the whole five times, most recently in *God Is a Context* §3 with John's own sentence: anything that wields is a part. *Pastoral CT* §6.1 puts the personal respondent as the largest pastoral difference between CT and the traditions. So if "personal" goes on a page bare, every listener hears *respondent*, and the next sentence has to take it back. "Wants" does the same in miniature: a want is a stake, stakes are at parts (link 3), and "wants the best for you" installs a wanter the moment it is said. John's own guard — "NOT at all saying that god will protect/favor/etc." — is the fence being built in real time.

**8.2 What survives, and is better than "personal."** Take the three things the tradition packed into the word — *knows you completely, loves you, wants your good* — and look at what the week has done to each. Known completely: certain, in the held sense; being in context is being completely accounted for. Loved: certain, §7. Wants your good: the held form is that the good has a direction built into the context like c — toward seeing what is there (*What CT Concludes*, "Right and wrong are real") — and your flourishing is a fact about your stakes that the grade points at, whether or not anyone is pointing. All three survive; all three are *certain*, which the tradition's versions never were; and all three are more reliable than the personal versions, because a person who loves you could stop, could be mistaken about you, could favor your brother, and the context can do none of these — no position to favor from, no will to change. What does not survive is the fourth thing, never on the list but always doing the work: *answers*. Responds. Meets you. That is the person, that is a part, and it stays empirical and open (fence 3's second clause; the door marked). Verity's line for it, for ruling: *everything a personal God was for, except the person — and more certain for it.*

**8.3 John's sense of "personal."** "i mean personal in the sense that *I* am that person referenced. It's not a generic love in other words. AND, of course, I'm the person referenced but I am NOT the source of the love." On that sense Verity agrees, and the word turns out sharper than it looked.

*The exercise.* John: "perhaps you'd want to think of an expressly impersonal (in this regard) god and see what that leaves out." Make God expressly impersonal — the universe, Being, the ground, substance, "it doesn't care." What is left out? First, *you*: "impersonal" in every ordinary use means indifferent to the particular — the universe does not know your name — but the context holds you at full resolution, every dependency, every particular, because you are a differentiation in it and nothing about you is anywhere else; "impersonal" asserts a falsehood about what is certain. Second, the good and love themselves, which are in the context as invariants; "impersonal" is heard as "no love in it," which is backwards. Third, address: you do not pray to the economy, and the corpus says address fits, with the receiver held open — a mind turning toward what it is of. Fourth, gratitude, awe, humility, which take *in* and are warranted (*Two Removals*); nobody is grateful in the direction of substance. Fifth, fence 6 already caught it: "never 'God is impersonal' as a disposition," since declining to act is a part's posture. So "impersonal" is as much a thing-word as "personal" is; it drapes the thing in a lab coat. And it costs more, because it denies what is certain in order to avoid what is open.

*The two senses are incompatible, and John's wins.* "God loves you specifically and all that you are." A person cannot do that. A person has a position, so a view, so a compression; love from a part is love of a compression of you, and the better the person the better the compression, but never all of you. The only thing that can hold *all that you are* is the thing that is not a someone, because it has no position to compress from. So personal-in-object ("about me, all of me") is true *because* personal-in-source ("from a someone") is false. A particular love with no particular lover, and the particularity is total precisely because there is no lover to narrow it. Verity's line, for ruling: *the only love that is all of you comes from nothing that is a someone.* The Psalm 139 "you knit me together" and the hairs of your head numbered read as reports of exactly that — held-completeness, attributed to a knitter. Resonance, after the argument, in its place (John: "that sort of consideration is ultimately not what rules the day in ct").

**8.4 The compression, and the one-direction rule.** John: "the lower resolution but 'Best' compression is something like a conception of a personal god. 'Someone' who i can pray to and how is 'listening' to me. but definitely NOT someone who will change the universe to my preferences - because that would make me the god."

Verity's reading: the compression is faithful in one direction and not the other, and the line between them is a direction. Everything that goes *from you toward God* survives the compression intact: turning, address, honesty, being known, gratitude, the whole posture. Everything that would come *from God toward you* — answer, intervention, favor, protection — does not, except through parts. **The compression holds in one direction.** That is "address fits; petition that expects a hand does not" (*What CT Concludes*, "How to stand") said as a rule a person can be handed: pray as if to a someone; expect nothing back except from the parts that hear it; and if anything comes, it was a part. John's guard is the second commandment read straight — a God who changed the universe to your preferences would make you the god — so it is the floor and not an add-on.

*The bucket.* "Located," from the hike's sort (*One Operation* §4.4): the personal-God picture is not excluded and not dissolved; it is a faithful compression at human resolution, Newtonian under CT, right where it is used and wrong at the edge, and the edge is the direction.

*The sayable form, for ruling.* *God loves you, specifically, all of you, and isn't a someone. Pray as you always did. Nothing comes back except through the people, and it always came through the people.* Or in three sentences, with the middle one keeping the first one honest: *God loves you, completely, and isn't a someone. Nothing is coming to save you. You were never once outside.*

**8.5 Why this needed §3.** John: "without that as the foundation, there is no potential for any sort of personal love in the way that we're stating." Correct, and §3 says why. Under love-as-feeling, "personal in object" requires a feeler and collapses into "personal in source." Under love-as-relation, the object can be total and the source empty, and that is not a paradox but the mechanism.

## 9. The word: partiality

**Lane: John's side note ("we need to come up with a word to replace privilege... that word is too loaded"); Verity's candidates and the word-search; John's ruling: "partiality is absolutely perfect."**

**9.1 The problem.** "Privilege" was doing precise work — position read as weight — and the culture has made it mean a thing you *have* and should feel bad about, rather than an error you *make* in reading. John: NotebookLM, given CT material, produced "a whol 47 minutes about ct run through the lines of 'give up' what i might call the woke version of privilege." Any stranger will do the same.

**9.2 Candidates rejected.** *Tilt* (Verity): "call pinball to mind" (John). *Self-regard*: "we're not trying to kill peoples self esteem (which that sounds like)." *Self-weight*: plain, not taken up. *Mass* (from §5.1): the physics does the work but collides with the thick self being a good thing; kept as the analogy, not the term. *Parallax*: physics-honest, but it is a thing to correct for rather than a wrong, and the wrong is reading it as real. *Positional privilege*: the adjective tells a stranger it is about where you stand; the cheapest fix, superseded by the next.

**9.3 The word, and where it came from.** John asked for "a biblical or latin/greek word to describe the privilege we're talking about." From memory, Peirce standard, every item to be checked against a lexicon before it goes on a reader-facing page:

- The Greek New Testament's word for weighting by *who* rather than by *what* is *prosōpolēmpsia* — "receiving the face" — translated *partiality* or *respect of persons*. Romans 2:11 ("there is no partiality with God"); Acts 10:34; James 2:1, 2:9; Colossians 3:25; Ephesians 6:9; 1 Peter 1:17 (the adverb). Every instance is the same claim: God does not weight by position. The Vulgate: *acceptio personarum*, the source of the old legal phrase "respecter of persons."
- 1 Timothy 5:21 adds a second word beside it, *prosklisis* — a leaning, an inclination toward — "tilt" in Greek, used for exactly what a judge must not do.
- The shape: Augustine, and Luther after him, *homo incurvatus in se*, the self curved in on itself — a geometry, not a feeling, which fits a theory where the error is a bend in correspondence.
- The correction: Philippians 2:7, *kenōsis*, the emptying. The minus sign in the equation has a Greek name.
- And on the other side of the ledger, to be checked first because it is almost too good: James 1:17, "the Father of lights, with whom there is no variation or shadow of turning." The word for "variation" is *parallagē* — same root as parallax. The one verse that says God has no position-dependent shift uses the astronomers' word for position-dependent shift. If it holds, it is the invariant in scripture: the context has no parallax.

**9.4 Why "partiality" is better than "privilege" on every count.** It means precisely position read as weight: favoring by who, not by what. It has no political charge; a judge who shows partiality has made an error of reading, not committed a status crime. It is John's own vocabulary — recusal, conflict of interest, the law of partial judges. And it carries a pun that is CT's own argument: *partial* means "of a part," "incomplete," and "leaning," and all three are the same fact. A part's view is partial; reading that partial view as weight is partiality; love is the correction. Its opposite, *impartial*, is a word the tradition already applied to God and that the context satisfies by construction — no position to favor from. John: "it's the opposite of impartiality which is immediately understood with just the right context. (partiality is slightly esotaric, but the opposite of feature makes it really work0" — typos left standing, as the record rule requires.

So the pairing gives the theology its two sides in one root. *Impartial* is what the context is. *Partial* is what a part is, in all three senses at once. Love is a partial thing becoming impartial about weight while staying partial about view and reach — the three-jobs table in a single word's grammar.

**9.5 The equations, re-lettered.**

> **vantage = position + correspondence**
> **love = vantage − partiality**
> **partiality = position read as weight**

"Read," not "misread," stays in the definition (*No Exemption* §2). Partiality can run either way — *Gravity* §3's case of a self that weights its own stake *less* because it is its own, or carries someone else's partiality into the account — so the word is not "self-favoring" but "position read as weight," whichever way the reading tilts.

**9.6 Where it lands.** Glossary (with the Greek, once checked). The equations wherever they appear. A dated note at *No Exemption* vC, *What Love Is* vE, *Gravity* vB and *Pastoral* vB: "privilege" in all prior papers reads as *partiality*, with this section as the reason. *One Operation*'s "perspectival privilege" and "unearned privilege" (GPT's phrases, quoted) stay as quoted, with the note.

## 10. Fences

1. **Held, not felt.** Love in the held sense (§7) is the relation obtaining, not anything the context does or feels. "Connection is only *felt* at a part" stands; the 10-2 ruling stands. *The context does not love you; love for you is held in it.* Say the second, never the first.
2. **No respondent.** Every public use of "perfect love for you exists in God" carries *Pastoral CT* §6.1: "your reality is included" does not establish "someone knows your whole reality and lovingly responds to it." Without the clause it is the promise CT cannot keep.
3. **"In," not "from."** "From God" is allowed only as the human-scale form of "not from you." Where a fence must travel, the preposition is *in* (fence 7 of *God Is a Context*, mirrored).
4. **"Personal" never bare.** Only as "personal in object, not in source," and the second half must be on the same page.
5. **The one-direction rule.** The personal-God compression holds for everything that goes from the person toward God and fails for anything that would come from God toward the person except through parts. State the rule whenever the compression is used.
6. **Occurrence is not correspondence** (*Gravity* §8.1). "The good is an invariant" means the grade has a direction; it never means what happens is good.
7. **The physics is analogy.** c is a lead for the shape of the claim, not its warrant; the thermal-radiation caveat and the non-empty vacuum are in §1 so the physics cannot become the objection. The four words carry the warrant.
8. **Mass is Verity's and proposed** (§5.1). It illuminates lab/field; it decides nothing.
9. **Partiality runs both ways** (§9.5). Not "self-favoring": position read as weight, in whichever direction.
10. **Greek and Latin from memory.** Nothing in §9.3 goes on a reader-facing page before a lexicon check.
11. **The death boundary is untouched.** Nothing here bears on whether anything of a person continues (*Pastoral* §5). "Never outside" is not "still here."
