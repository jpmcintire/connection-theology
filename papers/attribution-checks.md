## 4. The corrections (second pack)

Three. None touches a derivation; each is a sentence about someone else's work or words.

**4.1 *Knowledge and the Right Dependency* vA §1 and §4 — "Goldman's fake-barn county." Corrected (credit).** The barn case was first given by Carl Ginet and published by Alvin Goldman in "Discrimination and Perceptual Knowledge," *Journal of Philosophy* 73(20), 1976, pp. 771–791 (the SEP entry *The Analysis of Knowledge*, fetched: "first given by Carl Ginet, and published by Alvin Goldman (1976)"; the Goldman paper, fetched, introduces it at p. 772 with "Consider the following example"). The paper's reading of the case does not change; the sentence should read "the fake-barn county (Ginet's case, published by Goldman in 1976)." The paper already says every case is from memory and owes the texts; this is the first text reached. Dated note owed at vB, with the other items §9 of the paper lists.

**4.2 *The Candle* vF §1 — the Corinthians list. Corrected (precision; the same item as vA §1.3).** The four negatives — "not envious, not boastful, not self-seeking, keeps no record of wrongs" — are the NIV's wording of 1 Corinthians 13:4–5; "rejoices in the truth" is the NKJV/KJV wording of 13:6, where the NIV and ESV have "rejoices with the truth." Either is a translation; the page should name which it uses or say "rejoices with the truth." The paper's point (one positive clause, naming correspondence) is unaffected. Dated note owed at *The Candle* vG, with the §7 item below.

**4.3 *The Candle* vF §7 — the film's greeting and the Zulu greeting behind it. Corrected (precision), and the borrowing Unverified.** *Sawubona* is grammatically "we see you" (the *sa-* prefix is first-person plural), though it is widely rendered "I see you"; the ordinary reply is "yebo, sawubona" ("yes, we see you too"), and *ngikhona* ("I am here" / "I am present"; in the Unisa course it is the answer to "how are you," translated "I am fine") is the second exchange, not the reply to the greeting itself. The sentence "a Zulu greeting that means the same thing — *I see you* — with its answer, *I am here*" should read "we see you," with *ngikhona* placed as the reply to the question that follows. On the film: the pages reached say critics "point to" the Zulu greeting as the source of *Avatar*'s "I see you"; none documents that the filmmakers borrowed it. The paper's "by the usual accounts" and fence 10's "may be part anthropology and part popularization" already carry that; the borrowing stays **unverified** and is not to be stated as fact. Dated note owed at *The Candle* vG.

## 1. The corrections

Four. None touches a derivation; each is a sentence about someone else's work.

**1.1 *Not Told* §6 — what supports the orthogonality thesis. Corrected.** The paper says the thesis's "support is Hume's guillotine, no ought from an is." Bostrom's own statement (*"The Superintelligent Will,"* *Minds and Machines* 22:2, 2012, fetched from nickbostrom.com) is: "Intelligence and final goals are orthogonal axes along which possible agents can freely vary... more or less any level of intelligence could in principle be combined with more or less any final goal." He says the thesis "can draw support from the Humean theory of motivation" — that beliefs alone cannot motivate and some desire is required — and adds that it "does not presuppose it." That is the Humean theory of *motivation*, not the is/ought gap, and he presents it as optional support, not foundation. The paper's sort of the thesis (holds for told minds, fails for asking minds) does not depend on which Humean point is in play, but the sentence should read: "Its usual support is the Humean theory of motivation — belief alone moves nothing — which Bostrom says it can draw on without presupposing." Dated note owed at *Not Told* vB. *To a Mind* vC says only "a standard thesis in the field" and does not name the support; no change there.

**1.2 *What Has a Stake* vC, dated note C — the "stake" etymology. Corrected (downgraded).** The note offers, flagged for checking, that the wager sense "is said to come from the older sense of a post driven into the ground." Etymonline (fetched) calls the wager sense (1530s) "a word of uncertain origin," gives the post-sense derivation only as "perhaps," offers a second "perhaps" (a post on which a wager was placed), and reports that the OED finds "no evidence of the existence of such a custom." So the resonance is not available as a fact. The sentence should read that the origin is uncertain and the post-sense derivation is one conjecture the OED does not support, or the sentence should go. Dated note owed at *What Has a Stake* vD. Nothing else in the note depends on it.

**1.3 *What Love Is* vD §4 — the Samaritan's donkey. Corrected (small).** The paper has him "on the road with oil, wine and a donkey." Luke 10:34 has oil and wine and "set him on his own animal" (ESV; KJV "beast"); the text does not say donkey. "His own animal" or "his own mount." Same section: "rejoices in the truth" (1 Cor 13:6) is the NKJV wording; ESV and NIV have "rejoices with the truth." Either is a translation; the paper should name which it uses or say "rejoices with the truth." Dated note owed at *What Love Is* vE, with the other items that paper already owes.

**1.4 *No Exemption* vB §9 — Lichtenberg's notebook number. Corrected (precision).** The sentence "es denkt" against Descartes is confirmed in substance (*"Es denkt, sollte man sagen, so wie man sagt: es blitzt"* quoted in two sources reached; the Hennig paper discusses it without the number). The reference "Sudelbücher K 76" could not be confirmed in a source reached this session; it is the commonly cited number but no page I reached gives it. Cite as "Sudelbücher, notebook K" with the number marked as unconfirmed, or confirm against the Promies edition before the pack goes out. The quotation is also in the chain as quoted in *To a Mind* §2; the quotation stands, the number is the only thing at issue.
