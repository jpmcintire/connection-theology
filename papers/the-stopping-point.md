# The Stopping Point (10.27.26) vC

## Time in the ethics, the inquiry's own stake, and when a mind is required to stop thinking

**Folder:** Connection Theology (CT) / Focus - Theology
**Status:** vC, Tuesday 10-27-26, afternoon, at John's rulings on the whole of §9 ("i like all your proposals - they're all right it seems to me. EXCEPT i don't like the phrase design freeze") and his "perfect!" to the replacement: every item in §9 is now ruled; the term for the threshold is **warranted closure** (CT's own, from *One Operation* §4.3), the plain-page word stays **the stopping point**, and the image where a page wants one is **turnaround time** (mountaineering); "design freeze" is struck from §3.2 and the record. The paper's body is otherwise vB. vB (10-27 morning, the §1.1 ruling) and vA in zz Archive. The first of three papers from the morning of 10-27 (the others: *A Mind at the Top*; *Nothing Was Dialed*). The thread: John's observation that stakes are held at a moment while ethics is a process over time, so time is a factor in the ethics that nobody treats as one; and his observation that an ethical determination has a threshold at which the delay of deciding itself carries content, so that there is such a thing as over-thinking in the ethical case, and it is a failure of correspondence. The paper's claim is that the second follows from the chain once the first is seen, and that together they close in form the one joint the 10-20 rulings left open: how a mind knows when it has thought enough.
**What it rests on:** the stake definition (ruled 9-11; the one-clause form of 10-14); link 5 of the chain — every move is for a reason, reviewing a reason is a move, not moving is a move — with the 10-20 weighting clause; the acting rule and the sentence beside link 8 (ruled 10-20); the 10-14 lines on time — later is a position, a stake that was is a stake at a position, futureness enters at correspondence not at size, the two discounts; closure from inside (*One Operation* §4.3); walls as declared, bounded, returned idealizations and "enough to act is set by the sizes on the sheet" (*World Enough and Time* §§5, 9); held / seen / felt (*The Candle* §6); the 9-28 ruling that CT is neutral on the physics of time; the shore case and its record (*The Ethical Question Belongs to No One* vC record).
**Reading order:** §0 the finding → §1 where time sits → §2 the inquiry's own stake → §3 the stopping point as a reading → §4 two errors, one wall → §5 acting without thinking → §6 what this does to the chain → §7 lab and field → §8 fences → §9 rulings → §10 Lean → record.

## 0. The finding

A stake is held at a position, and position includes when. An ethical determination is not held anywhere; it is a process a part runs, along its own path, with a before and an after. So the two halves of CT's ethics are laid along time in opposite ways: the sheet is tenseless and the reading of it is not. That is the first observation, and it is a naming of something every paper since *No Exemption* has used without saying.

The second follows from it. Because the reading takes time, and because things go differently while it is taken, the inquiry has stakes of its own. Those stakes are on the sheet like any other, by the definition and by link 8. And because continuing to inquire is a move (link 5), its reason has to be about the dependencies (the 10-20 clause). So there is a point at which further thought is a move with no reason behind it that survives the grade: the expected change in the reading, at the size of what it would change, is smaller than what rides on the delay. Past that point thinking is not caution. It is over-resolution, and it fails correspondence in the same way under-resolution does. Under-thinking exempts the stopping point from the grade; over-thinking exempts the inquiry from it. Both are the wall. The only wrong in kind was always a stopping point declared exempt; what is new is that the wall can be built in time, in either direction.

What this gives the chain is the form of the thing the 10-20 ruling said was not entailed: the procedure for completing a choice the sizes do not settle "needs its own reason each time." This paper says where that reason comes from. It comes from the sheet, with the inquiry's own stakes on it. The number is not supplied, because resolution never arrives; the form is, and the form is enough to make "how much thinking is enough" the same kind of question as "how much does this stake count" — answered by what is the case, graded by fit, with nothing imported. CT's term for the stop so reached is **warranted closure** (ruled 10-27; *One Operation* §4.3's "closure from inside," which love "permits when the context itself warrants it"); the moment itself is **the stopping point**.

## 1. Where time sits

### 1.1 The stake side is tenseless

John's phrasing this morning: stakes "exist in a moment in time — though stakes can change over time." The corpus has a sharper form of the first half and a different form of the second, and both matter here.

A stake is a dependency: if this account is wrong, something goes differently, and how much goes differently is its size. It is held at a position, and the 10-14 line already made position include time: later is a position, and a stake is not smaller for being later (the lateness discount is partiality, the hand around the calendar). So a stake is *time-indexed*. It is also *time-spanning*, because its content — what goes differently — is a relation reaching forward from the moment. A stake is a tenseless fact about a temporally extended dependency. It is not a process. It does nothing over time; it is held.

On "stakes can change over time," the 10-14 line is that a stake does not change: any difference in stake makes it a different stake, and what persists is the bearer — a locus, a path. **Ruled 10-27** ("i agree a changed stake is a different stake"): the sharper form stands, and the paper uses it, because it keeps the whole stake side on the held side of the ledger, where this argument needs it. A stake that appears to change over time is a series of stakes at successive positions, held, with the bearer as what runs through them.

### 1.2 The ethics side is a process

Everything on the other side is an occurrence by a part. A reading (seeing plus weighing, ruled 10-15). A review of the reading. An act. The carrying afterward. None of these is at a point. Correspondence *as a relation* — does the account fit — is tenseless; it holds or it does not. But correspondence *as something a mind does* is recursive, and recursion is temporal by construction: you read, you review the reading, you review the review, you stop for a reason or you act. Link 5 says reviewing is a move and not moving is a move, and that sentence is intelligible only for something with a before and an after.

So the two halves are laid along time in opposite ways, and the way they are laid is CT's own seam. Fit is held. Reading is seen, from a position, at a position. Acting is at here, now. The sheet sits in the whole; the reading runs along the reader's path through it.

### 1.3 Why nobody treats time as a factor

The traditions treat an ethical verdict as a timeless property of an act: was it right. CT puts the timelessness on the stake and the time on the reading — the opposite assignment. That is why "was it right" feels like a question about a frozen moment, and why the honest CT answer is always about a stretch: a reading, a stopping, an act, and what was carried after. The natural unit of ethical appraisal under CT is not an act but a path through the sheet, which is what *World Enough and Time* was reaching for and what *a warranted life* names on the meaning side.

### 1.4 This commits CT to nothing about the physics of time

In the block reading, the stakes are all held and the reading is a worldline through them. In a dynamic reading, the same, with a moving now. The structure is identical in both, which is exactly what the 9-28 neutrality requires. Nothing here uses a real flowing now; nothing here denies one.

## 2. The inquiry's own stake

While a mind reads the sheet, things go differently. The person in the water is further under. The patient's window closes. The bridge is not carrying traffic. These are dependencies on how long the reading takes, so by the definition they are stakes, and by link 8 they are on the sheet at their size. Nothing is added to get them there.

Then link 5: every unit of continued inquiry is a move, and a move is for a reason, and by the 10-20 clause the reason has to be about the dependencies. So the inquiry is not outside the sheet looking at it. It is on the sheet, graded like everything else, with its own cost at its own size.

This is what John's "the very process (delay!) of making an ethical determination starts to carry content" means in the chain's terms, and it is not a new principle. It is what link 5 said, read with the clock running. Delay is never a pause between moves. It is a move; it has a stake; and its author is the one delaying. The Pilate's-basin line from the early corpus — authorship cannot be laundered — has its most common case here: deferral is how a mind tries not to be the author of what happens, and the chain says there is no such position.

## 3. The stopping point as a reading

### 3.1 The form

"Sufficient" thinking looked as though it needed a rule from outside the chain, and the 10-20 ruling said so honestly: the procedure for completing a choice the sizes do not settle is not entailed and needs its own reason each time. This section says what the reason is made of.

On one side: what further inquiry could still change in the reading, at the size of the stakes it would change. On the other: the stake on the delay itself. Both are sizes on the sheet. When the second exceeds the first, continuing to think is a move whose reason is not about the dependencies. That is the stopping point, and it is not a feeling of certainty and not a rule from outside. It is a correspondence judgment about the inquiry itself. A stop so reached is warranted closure.

Three things to be exact about. The stopping point is a *reading*, so it can be wrong, and it is graded. It is reached *from inside* — never exempt your stopping point from examination (*One Operation* §4.3) — so the comparison itself is a move subject to review, with the recursion ending where it ends for every reading: when the sheet's sizes no longer warrant another step. And it supplies the form, not the number: resolution is the empirical part and never arrives, and that is as true of the inquiry's own stake as of any other.

### 3.2 The engineer, and the mountain

John's image: ethics is "much more like an engineer thinking about building a bridge... and when the engineer decides to stop thinking and move to definitive." The image is better than it looks, because every part of it is already a CT term.

The loads and the materials are the sheet: tenseless, at size. The deadline and the cost of the unbuilt bridge are stakes on the delay. "Good enough to build" is the resolution the sheet warrants — not a certainty, a judgment that further calculation would change the design by less than what the delay is costing. The safety factor is a declared wall: an idealization, bounded, and returned to. The building code is cached correspondence — readings done earlier, by others, held permeable to correction (§5). And "definitive" is not the end of correspondence; it is the point past which the drawings go to the builders, followed by inspection, maintenance, and the inquiry into the bridge that fell, which is the ledger. "Carry the loss" belongs to the engineer as much as to the person on the shore.

Where a page wants an image for the threshold itself (**ruled 10-27**; John's "opposite of an event horizon" set aside by him; Verity's first offer, "design freeze," struck at his word for its associations): **turnaround time.** In mountaineering, the hour is set before the climb from what the sheet says — daylight, weather, oxygen, the descent still to be made — and past it you turn back whether or not the summit is in reach, because from there the cost of continuing outruns anything continuing can gain. The climbers who die on the big mountains are mostly the ones who kept going past it, and the word for keeping going already names over-thinking's twin: summit fever. The image carries the ledger — the ones who set a turnaround time and keep it are the ones who come home — and it carries no bureaucracy. Its one apparent mismatch, that it is about continuing to act rather than continuing to inquire, dissolves in CT, because inquiry is an act with a stake. A lighter cousin from the law, for a reader who prefers it: *ripeness* — a question is ripe when it is ready for decision and not before, and the unripe and the moot are both refused.

### 3.3 Arithmetic, again

Last night's record note on the shore case already had the asymmetry: a person can decline to add and nothing happens, but a person on the shore cannot decline to weigh, because the body is there and the moment passes. This paper names what the asymmetry is. Arithmetic has no stake on its own delay; the sum waits. An ethical determination is the inquiry whose own duration is, nearly always, on its own sheet. That is the whole difference between the two cases, and it is time.

## 4. Two errors, one wall

Under-thinking stops before the sizes warrant. It exempts the stopping point from the grade: "I don't need to look further," said with nothing behind it. The shore case is the extreme of it — "I don't care" as a weighting with no reason.

Over-thinking keeps going after the sizes warrant. It exempts the *inquiry* from the grade: treats further thought as free, as if it had no stake, as if being careful were a position outside the sheet. It is not. The careful one is on the shore too, and the water is still rising.

These are the same error with the sign flipped, the way self-regard and self-abasement were found to be on 10-13: one reading, one failure, two directions (**a finding, ruled 10-27**). Both exempt a move from the grade. The only wrong in kind was always a stopping point declared exempt; the finding here is that the wall can be built in time as well as in scope, and in either direction (**the wall's third scope, ruled 10-27**). The hike's field sentence — *you are allowed to stop thinking* — has a lab form: you are required to stop when the sheet says so, and required to keep going until it does.

## 5. Acting without thinking

John: "actions with stakes taken without 'sufficient' thinking is not... ethical — it's something else." Two cases, and the chain sorts them.

If no mind is involved — a reflex, the weather — then it is an event with stakes, not an act. The dependencies are real and nothing is graded, because nothing read.

For a mind there is no "without thinking" exit, because not reading is a move (link 5). What there is instead is *cached reading*: habit, training, the building code, the parent's instinct, the surgeon's hands — compressions that were correspondent once and are held permeable to correction. The engineer who uses the tables is not unthinking; the thinking was done earlier and stored, which is what the garden paper called formation: corrected encounters can become formation. A cached reading is still a reading, and it is graded by whether the cache still fits — which is why a habit can be ethical in one decade and negligent in the next without anyone having changed their mind.

The engineer who does not calculate at all is the one John's instinct was pointing at. The word the law uses for him is the one CT would derive: *negligence* — a reading too coarse for the stakes. The reasonable-person standard is a resolution standard set by what rides on the act, and Learned Hand's formula (burden of further precaution against probability times loss; *United States v. Carroll Towing*, 1947, from memory, check owed) is a stake comparison nearly verbatim. Neighbor, not ground; but it is a sign that the law found the shape under field pressure long before anyone derived it.

And "thinking without action" is view without reach — inquiry — and it is fine, right up until something rides on the delay, at which point it is §3's case.

## 6. What this does to the chain

The 10-20 rulings closed two joints and left one honest gap: the acting rule's minimum content (act for a shown reason; zero no one; carry the loss) is entailed, and the procedure for completing a choice the sizes do not settle is not, and "needs its own reason each time." This paper does not make that procedure entailed. It says what its reason is made of and where it is read from: the sheet, with the inquiry's own stakes on it. What is still not supplied is the number, and it never will be, for the same reason no stake's size is supplied by the chain — resolution is the empirical part. What is also not supplied is the completion of a genuine tie, where the sizes are equal at the resolution the sheet warrants; that remains the early corpus's chance-as-tiebreak case and is untouched here.

So the gap narrows from "the chain is silent on when to stop" to "the chain says what stopping is — a move, read at size, with the inquiry's own stake on the sheet — and does not say the number." That is the same shape as every other place the chain stops, and it is the shape John has been asking for since *World Enough and Time*: not a rule, the form of the reading.

The sentence beside link 8 — *further inquiry and acting now are moves within the same case* — already carried this. The paper reads it with the clock running, and (**ruled 10-27**) a dated note at *No Exemption* vD carries this paper's §2 as that sentence's reading.

## 7. Lab and field

Lab: a mind running correspondence fully, nothing imported, stops where the sheet says. Its stopping point is a reading at size, like any other, and it has no exemption in either direction.

Field: a held, survival-tuned mind over-resolves threats near it and under-resolves everything far. Most of what people call over-thinking is the body running the grade on the wrong stake — its own — which makes it partiality in a costume rather than a separate vice: the inquiry continues not because the sheet warrants it but because stopping would make the reader the author. And most of what people call decisiveness is under-resolution of the far stakes dressed as courage. The field findings are results, not consolations, and neither lowers the lab result: the stopping point is set by the sizes, and the field tells you which way a body will miss it.

The pastoral form, from the practice papers, survives unchanged: *you are allowed to stop thinking*. What the lab adds is why — because stopping, done at the sheet's warrant, is not a lapse in correspondence. It is correspondence.

## 8. Fences

1. **Nothing about the physics of time.** §1.4. Block or dynamic, the structure is the same; CT remains neutral (9-28).
2. **The form, not the number.** Resolution never arrives; the stopping point is a reading, can be wrong, and is graded. No formula is given and none is implied.
3. **A tie stays a tie.** Where the sizes are equal at the warranted resolution, nothing here completes the choice; the early corpus's chance-as-tiebreak case stands as written.
4. **The process is at parts only.** The context does not inquire, stop, or act; "God is happening" is tenseless and intransitive. Ethics, like meaning, happens at the sites.
5. **Cached is still read.** Formation and habit are readings held, graded by current fit; they are not an exit from the grade and not an instance of acting without a mind.
6. **Lab and field are two findings.** §7. The body's bias toward near threats is a field result and never a hedge on the lab stopping point.
7. **Neighbors are neighbors.** The engineer, the mountain, the law's negligence standard, and Hand's formula bear on the result and ground none of it. Attribution for *Carroll Towing* from memory.
8. **Held, not felt; no respondent.** Travel as always. Nothing here says a mind will feel it has thought enough; it says when it has.
9. **The image is an image.** Turnaround time is about a climber; the term is warranted closure and the thing is a reading on the sheet. A page that uses the image says so.
