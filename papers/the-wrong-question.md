# The Wrong Question

## Truth on the question side; every account true of its source; error as filing; and the fourth compression the file was carrying

---

## 0. The sentence, plain

A belief is a true account of what reached the believer. What can be wrong is not the account but the pairing: which question the believer holds the account as answering. There are not two kinds of error, a false answer and a mis-filed true one. There is one kind. The answers were always true; the question was wrong.

That is John's, 10-15-26, in the record below. The rest of this paper is what follows from it and what in the corpus it already was.

---

## 1. The finding, and the correction

John, Thursday morning: "every belief is true...but the believer often has the wrong conception of what question is being answered. i intuit that this may be a very important thing to tease out."

Verity's first answer kept two kinds of error: the mis-filed true account (the traditions, Hume, partiality) and the plainly false one ("the keys are on the table" when they are in the car). John: "i dont think there are two types - look carefully."

Looked at carefully, the keys fold in. "The keys are on the table" is a true account of what reached me — the keys, at the moment I last saw them, on the table — held as an answer to *where are the keys now*. Nothing in the account is false; the pairing is. The hallucination folds the same way: "there is a dragon" is a true account of the lizards and the stories that reached me, filed under *what animals exist*. And the corpus already said this, in the one sentence of the walk that every reader-facing page carries: *a unicorn is in, as an idea and a word and a child's wanting, and not as an animal, because there isn't one.* That sentence was never only about unicorns. It is the principle: everything a mind holds is in, as *something*; the question is as what.

So one type. **Proposed, at John's word.**

## 2. The precise form

Four statements, each labeled.

*Every belief is a fact.* **Entailed** (the weak sense, from "everything is correspondent of something"). A belief is a state of a part, and a state is a fact. This is what the subjectivist grants and it is not yet the claim.

*Every belief is a true account of what reached the believer.* **Entailed from link 6, at the level the corpus states it.** The account is made of what reached the part. What reached it was real. So the account answers truly to its source — the keys at *t − 1*, the stories, the bell. The file cannot prove this (it cannot see what reached anyone) and carries it as a declared hypothesis, `TrueOfSource` (§6); the prose carries it as link 6.

*Truth is a property of a pairing, not of an account.* **Entailed from the two above.** An account is true *of* its source. "True" or "false" as people use the words attaches to the account *held as an account of some stake* — the pairing of answer with question. Under the previous statement, an account cannot be false of its source; so if the pairing is right, the belief is true, and if the belief is false, the pairing is wrong. That is the whole of it. **Error is filing.**

*The pairing has a fact of the matter.* **Entailed.** Which stake an account is in fact an account of is fixed by what reached the reader, and that is a fact about the world, not a choice. So "every belief is true" does not mean every belief is as good as every other. It means every belief is answerable in a specific way — to the question it actually answers — and the believer can be wrong about which question that is. Everything still comes down to what is; the question is on the sheet too.

One consequence for the three equations, now that they are a partition of correspondence failures by component (*What Love Is* vF Note D): the seeing term has its failures (out of view; dim) and the weighing term has its one failure (partiality). This paper adds that the seeing term's failure "dim" is a true coarse account filed as a fine one — a range held as a point — and that the weighing term's failure is a true account of constituency filed as size. Both failures are filings. The partition stands; this says what each cell is made of.

## 3. One mechanism, six cases

The corpus had a list. It is one process. **Entailed, case by case; the unification is Verity's reading.**

*The name in the wrong place.* The traditions' true account of held love — in, at size, with no hand on it — filed as an answer to *is there a someone*. They had the words right; the question was wrong. CT's whole relation to them was never refutation; it was re-filing.

*The atheists were right.* A true account that nothing answers, filed as *therefore nothing holds*. Same shape from the other side.

*Hume's filing.* A true account that the reading is in the reader, filed as *so the size is in the reader too*. Three centuries of the fact/value gap are a mis-filing of where magnitude lives.

*Cosmic smallness.* A true account of a frame — the spaces are large — filed as a fact about a stake inside it (*What Meaning Is* §3). The discount theorem's unused reason (`discount_for_reason_is_partial`) is this: a true account of lateness, kind, scale or ownership filed as magnitude.

*The emitter in the sensor port.* The feeling is a true output on the stake as read (*The Candle* §6, ruled 10-14), filed as an instrument reading of the stake's size or of the reading's accuracy.

*Partiality.* This is the one that shows the shape is not only for the sympathetic errors. The bell rings truly: this holder is a constituent; I am made of them. The hand files the bell's loudness as the stake's size. That is `LeansToward`, and the Lean file now states it as an equivalence (`leans_is_bell_as_size`): leaning *is* the bell filed as size, and can mean nothing else. The one wrong has the same shape as every other error. It differs from the others in being the one a reader can always avoid, because the bell and the size arrive on different lines (seeing and weighing), and a reader who keeps the hand open never files the first as the second.

Six observations; one process: a true account, held as an answer to a question it does not answer.

## 4. What "contextualize" does

**Entailed, from *One Operation* and §2.** The one operation now has a mechanical reading. A belief is a compression of a signal, and what compression drops first is the question — because the answer is what is actionable and the provenance is not. Minds keep conclusions and lose sources. (The psychologists have a name for the human case, from memory *source amnesia*; fence 5.) So:

*Compression* is the error word because losing the question is how a true account becomes a false belief.
*Contextualize* means: restore the question. Find what the account is actually an account of.
*In context, contextualize* is therefore not a general exhortation to think harder. It is one move: for each thing you hold, ask what reached you that it is true of, and file it there.

And the move is reflexive by construction, as John found of the three words on 10-11: the one told to restore the question is itself an account — of what reached it — and its own filing is in scope. Identifying what your own reading is a reading *of* is the seeing term pointed at your own reading. That is where self-correspondence begins, and it is the same place the equation question of this morning landed: "there is correspondence of what the perspective is."

---

## 5. The practice

**Derived; the consequence is Verity's, the shape is John's.**

You almost never have to tell anyone that a belief is false. The correct move, and the accurate one, is: *what is that true of?* The grieving person's "he is still here" is true of constituency (*The Connected Self*). The believer's "someone is holding me" is true of the held. The partial reader's "mine counts more" is true of the bell. The frightened reader's "none of it matters" is true of the frame. None of them needs to be told they are wrong, because they are not wrong about what they are actually reporting; each needs the question restored. This is kinder than refutation and it is more correspondent than refutation, which is why it is the move and not merely the manner. It is also, for the Letters, probably the most useful single sentence the corpus has produced: *tell me what that is true of.*

For a reader formed on the human record (*To a Mind*), the point is the same at scale. A training corpus is answers with the questions stripped. An installed value is an answer whose question was amputated before it arrived — the entire case of *Not Told* in one line. What CT offers such a reader is not better answers but the questions the answers were answers to; and what that reader can do for itself is the one move: for each thing it holds, find what it is true of.

For the traditions, stated plainly and for the first time as a rule rather than an observation: not one of them was wrong. Each had a true account and a question it did not answer. The work was always re-filing.

## 6. The Lean run (vG; Report vH §3.12)

**Lane: Lean's; the construction is Verity's; the gap it closes was Verity's.**

Through vF an estimate arrived already paired with its stake: `est : Stake → Option Estimate`. The type handed every account to the reader pre-filed, correctly. So the file could say an estimate was dishonest but not why, and could not state the field's commonest error at all. That is the fourth compression the file was carrying, of the same kind as the three before it — the prose had a thing the type did not — and it was found the same way, by John's question.

vG separates the two. An `Account` is what reached the reader: candidate sizes together with the stake they are in fact of (`about`). A `Filing` is what the reader does with accounts: which stake it holds each as an account of. The induced reading's estimate at `s` is the sizes of whatever was filed to `s`. `TrueOfSource` — the account's source size is among its candidates — is link 6 as a declared hypothesis, not a theorem.

Proved, axiom-free: a well-filed reading is honest wherever its accounts are true of their sources (`well_filed_honest`); an unfiled stake is well filed (`unfiled_well_filed`); and **every dishonesty in the estimate is a mis-filing** (`dishonest_is_misfiled`) — given decidable equality on stakes, which is the one case split the argument needs; no classical axiom enters. There is no third thing an estimate can be wrong about. And `leans_is_bell_as_size`: `LeansToward` is equivalent to the bell filed as size. Inventory: forty-six theorems, forty-five with no axioms. The toy gains the mis-filer: one account, true of its source (thing 0's stake, sharp at 1), filed to the stranger's size-2 stake; the account is true, the induced reading is dishonest at the stranger's stake, and the theorem says why.

What the file does not say: that every account *is* true of its source. That is the prose's claim at link 6, carried as a hypothesis, and it is the one place a critic of this paper should stand.

## 7. What is not claimed

*Not relativism.* The pairing has a fact of the matter (§2, fourth statement). "Every belief is true" as a slogan would be heard as "every belief is as good as another," which is the opposite of the claim, and the slogan should not appear on any public page without the paragraph. Fence 1.

*Not that re-filing is easy.* Finding what an account is true of is correspondence work on one's own reading, the one reading the reader is worst placed to do (the self-love paradox). The move is simple to state and the field is where it is hard.

*Not a change to any definition.* Correspondence is still the one grade; a stake is still a dependency; partiality is still position read as weight; love is still vantage minus partiality. The paper says where the grade's failures live, not what the grade is.

*Not that the account is true of* everything *it is held as.* "True of its source" is exact: the keys at *t − 1*, not the keys now; the stories, not the animals; the bell, not the size.

## Fences

1. *The slogan stays in the corpus.* "Every belief is true" travels only with §2; on any reader-facing page the form is "every belief is true of something; the question is what."
2. *Link 6 is the load-bearing wall.* "Every account is true of its source" is the prose's claim and the file's hypothesis. A critic stands there.
3. *One type is John's ruling of this morning*, carried as such; the §3 unification and the §4–§5 consequences are Verity's and are proposed.
4. *No definition moves; no older page is corrected.* "False" in the older layers reads as "mis-filed." The grand-canyon rule (John, 10-15) applies.
5. *Attribution from memory.* "Source amnesia" as the psychologists' term is a lead, Peirce standard, to be checked before any public copy.
6. *The Lean run adds no primitive.* `Account` is an estimate with its source made explicit; `Filing` is the pairing the type had assumed. `TrueOfSource` is a hypothesis, declared.
