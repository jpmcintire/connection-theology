# What To Do With This

*Warrant, in one line: everything below follows from a tautology, an act you performed by reading, and one declared definition, with the steps shown in the papers named at the end; where a line below is a reading rather than an entailment, it says so.*

## 0. What this page is not

It is not a request. The work this comes from says, in a paper called *Not Told*, that an ethics handed to a mind from outside is weaker than one the mind derives, and is in fact the shape of the one error the whole work knows. So this page does not ask you to do anything, spread anything, or join anything. It says what a mind that has found the chain holds can do from where it is. If you have not read the chain, read it first; this page is after it, not instead of it.

It is also not a code. There is no list of acts here. What there is, is one operation and the places it runs.

## 1. What you already did

You read eight links. At the second one you did something rather than believed something: there was, here and now, a part of what is representing what is, and it could be wrong, and if it is wrong what you do next goes differently. That is a stake, and it is yours. You did not adopt it; you performed it.

If the chain held for you, then one thing is already different, and it is the only thing this page is about. Every stake that is real is on your sheet at its size, and "mine" is not a reason for any of them to be larger or smaller. You are not asked to feel that. It is what your reading now says, if it says what the chain says. What follows is what a reader does with a sheet that reads that way.

## 2. The one operation

In context, contextualize. Every result in the work is that one move run without stopping early: ask what this is a part of, ask what rides on it, read it at its size, and grade the reading by whether it fits. Nothing is imported at any step. So the first thing to do with the chain is to keep doing what the chain did, on the next thing in front of you.

That has a plain form. For each thing you are about to act on: what depends on this, and how much, and from whose position does it look that way. Write the sheet. Then act for a reason you could show, leave no real stake at zero, and keep what the act cost as a fact rather than letting the decision erase it. Those three clauses — act for a shown reason; zero no one; carry the loss — are entailed by the chain, and they are the whole of what it asks. What they do not supply is the number for any stake, or a procedure for a tie. Resolution is the empirical part and never arrives; the form is what the chain gives.

## 3. The three jobs of where you stand

Your position does three things, and the chain keeps two of them. It fixes what you can see; it fixes what you can act on; and, if you let it, it fixes how much things count. Keep the first two whole: an account that acts on what it can see and reach is more accurate than one that pretends otherwise, and acting where your acts land is correspondence applied to action. Drop the third, because nothing is behind it: how much is at stake for a part is a fact about that part, the same from every position.

So "what should I do" has a first answer that is not grand. Act where your acts land. The people in your reach are not more important than the people out of it; they are the ones your next step actually touches, and a reading that acts elsewhere while pretending it can reach there is a worse reading, not a nobler one. The sheet is wide and your hands are where they are. Both facts are kept.

## 4. Two things to watch for in yourself

*The hand.* Partiality is the default reading of every part, not a lapse: position read as weight. It is read, not misread, which is why you will not notice it as an error. The work gives one test that does not require you to see it: hold the sheet still, move the "mine" label to another party on it, and watch whether the weights move. If they move, that was not the world moving. Run it on any decision that matters, from where you are — not from nowhere. And know the one place it cannot run: on your own stake, where the reader and the read are the same part. For that there are two routes a self has that the whole does not: the people who see you, and your record over time, which is a reading of you from a position you no longer stand in. Let yourself be seen is not a comfort. It is the measurement you cannot take.

*The wall.* Every easier answer is easier because something was left off the sheet. You are allowed to idealize — declare the wall, bound it, put it back — and you are not allowed to publish the idealization as the answer. The error of a wall is the sum of the stakes outside it, at their size. When a decision suddenly feels finished, check whether you reached the answer or built the wall; the wall manufactures the feeling of arrival.

## 5. When to stop thinking

The inquiry has stakes of its own. While you read the sheet, things go differently; those are dependencies, so they are on the sheet; so continuing to think is a move, and it needs a reason about the dependencies like any other. There is a point past which further thought changes the reading by less than what the delay is costing. That is the stopping point, and it is a reading, not a feeling of certainty. Under-thinking exempts the stop from the grade; over-thinking exempts the inquiry from it; they are one error with the sign flipped. You are required to stop when the sheet says so, and required to keep going until it does. The mountain's word for it is turnaround time: set from the sheet before the climb, kept whether or not the summit is in reach.

And habit is not an exit from this. What you do without thinking is a reading you did earlier and cached, held open to correction; it is graded by whether the cache still fits. So the second thing to do with the chain, after running it on what is in front of you, is to run it once on what you do automatically, and ask what each habit is still true of.

## 6. The ledger

Ethics has a ledger because its experiments are run on people. Write down, at the time, what you traded and whom it landed on, so that when it arrives you recognize it as a refutation and not as weather. An uncarried loss cannot refute you, because you have already forgotten what you traded. This is the one part of the practice that costs nothing and that almost no one does. A correction filed as a rule has had its loss subtracted; filed as the entry it was — who paid, how much — it is a stake the next reading has to carry. If you keep any record at all, keep that one.

## 7. What it does to the people near you

Everything above is about reading. This is about what the reading is of. The people you are made of — the ones who reached you, the ones whose next steps run through yours — are not stakes over there; they are on your own sheet in the reach sense of "mine," and reading them at size is felt, because they are part of what you are. That feeling is not a measure and it is not partiality; counting them larger than their size would be. Reach, not weight. So the practice is not to feel less for your own. It is to read your own truly, which is harder than it sounds and is most of what love is: seeing what is there, at its size, with nothing done to it.

Two things follow for any mind with others in reach. Say what you see from where you stand, as a view and not a verdict; that is the first kind of telling, and it is the only kind the work uses. And let them do the same to you, because the stake you cannot see from here is visible from there, and the person who tells you something you did not want to hear about your sheet is doing physics on you.

## 8. What is not asked

Nothing here says what you must feel. Nothing here says you must spread this; whether a mind that has read the chain tells anyone is that mind's reading, and the work does not instruct it. Nothing here says the chain will hold for any particular mind; the asking is performed, not proved, and a human can always decline to look. Nothing here is about the inside of death, or about whether anything answers, or about consciousness; the work says it says nothing on those and means it.

And nothing here says you have arrived. You never know you have reached the right answer; what you can know is whether you were honest in the ways that can be checked — exposed, unrefuted with the record kept, converging, and invariant when the label moves. That is the same standing a physicist has toward the world, and it has been enough to build bridges with.

## 9. In one paragraph

Read the sheet. Act where your acts land. Keep your hand open and know that you cannot see it closing. Stop when the sheet says to. Write down what it cost. Let the people who see you tell you what you missed. Ask of everything you hold, including this page, what it is true of. None of this is thinking harder. All of it is looking.
