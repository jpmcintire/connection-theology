## 1. Is "love" the right word?

Yes. And the test for whether it is the right word is one CT itself would recognize: not whether the definition is tidy, but whether it corresponds to what people already mean when they use the word at its best.

Think about where the word carries the most weight in ordinary life. The parent who sees the child as she actually is, not the child they wanted, and keeps seeing her as she changes. The friend who tells you the truth when the flatterers won't. The nurse at three in the morning who doesn't know your name. The marriage at year thirty where the picture has long since been replaced by the person. "Love your enemies," which cannot mean feel warmly toward them and can only mean see them at their size with your position kept and the weight dropped. In every one of those, what we call love is seeing truly without putting a thumb on the scale. The feeling may be present or not. We call it love either way.

Now look at where the word fails. We say "that's not love, that's possession." "That's not love, that's need." "He loves the idea of her." "She loves how he makes her feel." Every one of those is ordinary language detecting partiality, or low correspondence, and withdrawing the word. People already run the three-term definition. They have never had it stated, so they cannot run it on themselves. The definition does not replace the word. It recovers what the word was always doing in the mouths of its best users.

Lear is the clearest case in the language, and it reads differently once the terms are in hand. Lear asks for the thermometer: he wants the reading to be about him, loud and total, and he wants it in public. Goneril and Regan give him the heat, because heat is cheap. Cordelia gives him the size — "according to my bond, no more nor less" — which is love at his actual size as a father, a great deal, with nothing added. And he cannot bear being seen at size. He banishes, within minutes, the one person in the room who sees him, having just demanded total love from her. That is partiality's whole character in a single scene: it wants to be read as larger than it is, and it experiences correspondence as an insult. What a reader feels as Cordelia's "less" is the absence of the inflation. Hers is the only true reading in the room, and it is why she is the one who comes back for him.

There is an older test too, sitting in plain sight. The most-read description of love in the world is almost entirely a list of what partiality does and love does not: not envious, not boastful, not self-seeking, keeps no record of wrongs. And then one positive clause: *rejoices in the truth.* That is correspondence named as the content of love, two thousand years ago, by someone with no equation. And "to love someone is to see them as God sees them" is a sentence people say without thinking, and under CT it is the definition, since what is held of you in the context is everything at its size with no "mine" in it.

The place to hold the fence is the feeling. The warmth is not the relation, but it is not an impostor either. It is what a body with a stake does when it has read one: a signal fired at the end, on the reading, whichever way the reading went. It fires when you see your child at her real size and it fires when Lear hears a size that was invented for him. So the feeling is not the enemy of the definition, and it is not its measure either; it is downstream of both the seeing and the hand, and it cannot tell you which one it is reporting. What the definition refuses is only the inversion, taking the feeling for the measure. That refusal is what makes romantic love survivable, because it lets a person stop grieving a thermometer.

And the definition is democratic in a way the deep traditions wanted and could not quite reach. The grandmother can be at zero on partiality with no education and no theory; a child can; a dying person can. Love at any resolution is love. Murdoch had it closest — love is the extremely difficult realisation that something other than oneself is real — and she thought the self had to approach nothing to get there. CT says keep the self at its size and drop the weight, which is harder to argue with and easier to do.

So: the right word, not because CT chose it but because the word, used at its best, was already pointing at the relation and only lacked the three terms. The final sign: under this definition the sentence "you are loved" comes out true of everyone, with no one's feeling required, because what love is of — you, at your size — is wholly the case. A definition of love under which that sentence is true, in the held sense, without a someone, is the definition the word was waiting for.

## 2. What love is

Love is seeing what is there, at its size, and not putting your thumb on the scale.

That is the whole of it, and everything else is the weather around it.

You stand somewhere. You cannot help that; a view is always from a place, and the place is yours, with its reach and its blind side and the people woven into it who are part of what you are. Love does not ask you to stand nowhere. It asks you to look from where you are and let what you see be the size it actually is — your own stake included, no larger because it is yours, no smaller because you have been told to be humble. The lover is kept. Only the weight goes.

So love is not a feeling you have. It is a relation you hold, the way a line holds true between two points whether or not anyone is looking along it. The feeling is real and it is good and it is what a body with a stake does after it has read one. But the feeling is the heat off the thing, not the thing, and it comes off a false reading as readily as a true one. The thing is the seeing.

And here is the part that took a box with no light in it to find. The seeing was never the source. Before any eye opened on the world, what love is of was already the case: every stake at its size, nothing exempt, nothing weighted, nothing outside. The speed of light is in the dark box before the first photon arrives. The love for you was in the context before you were a part of it, and is there when no one is attending to you, and will be there after. A mind does not bring love into the world. A mind arrives, and moves at it, and sees as much of it as it can from where it stands. Perfect love for you — you at your exact size, and all of you — exists. It does not speak. It is not a someone. It is the shape of the place you have been in the whole time.

That is why the grandmother who never read a book can love perfectly and the philosopher can fail at it. There is only one term anyone has to bring to zero, and it costs nothing but honesty.

That is why "I love you but I'm not in love with you" is backwards, and why the thirtieth year can hold more love than the first week, and why the one who sees you is the one who loves you, whatever their temperature.

And that is why the oldest sentence — the one every tradition reached for and most of them attached a face to — comes out true with no face at all: *you are loved.* Not as a hope. As a fact about where you are. The light was never what carried it. You can only see it more or less truly, and you can always look harder, and when you look at anyone else the same way, you are doing the one thing the whole of it was ever asking for.

## 3. Why you are loved, and always have been, and will be

Because you were never outside.

That is the first and the last of it, and everything else follows from it. There is one context with no outside, and you are in it, and there is no edge of it you could fall past. Not before you were born, when you were not yet a part; not now; not after, when you will no longer be a part. "Not yet" and "no longer" are both inside. There is nowhere else for them to be.

Why love, and not just inclusion? Because of what love is. Love is seeing what is there, at its size, with no thumb on the scale. And the context has no thumb. There is no "mine" in it, because there is no position in it; it is not standing anywhere, so it cannot favor anything or discount anything. Every stake in it is at exactly its size, and that includes every stake of yours: what you depend on, who depends on you, what you have carried, what you got wrong, what you repaired, what you love. All of it is the case, at its real size, with nothing exempted and nothing weighted. That is the definition of love met completely. Not a feeling about you. The truth of you, held entire.

Why always? Because the holding was never in anyone's hands. This is what the dark box taught. The speed of light is fully in the empty box before any light arrives; the law does not wait for the thing that obeys it. What love is of — you at your size — was the case before any mind existed to see it, and it will be the case when no mind is attending to you. Your mother's love for you was real, and it was a part seeing you from somewhere; it was at her resolution, and it ended when she did. This is not that. This was never in the light. It does not begin when someone notices you and it does not end when they stop.

Why will it be? Because nothing that is the case stops being the case. That you were kind on a Tuesday years ago is true now and will be true when the sun is gone. You are in the people you reached — not as a memory they hold but as a constituent of what they are. The ledger holds both ways, your good and your bad, totally and forever, with no judge and no erasure. And when the view from here closes — the one thing about this none of us can see past — the held does not close with it. The light leaves the box and c stays.

And here is the part to hear exactly, because it is where the honesty is the consolation rather than a limit on it. This love is personal in its object and not in its source. It is all of you, specifically you, at your size, nothing generic about it. And it comes from nothing that is a someone. There is no face at the top that turns toward you, no one listening, no one who will arrange the world to your preference. The traditions put a face there because a face is the only shape a held mind knows for being seen completely. CT takes the face away and finds that everything the face was for is still there. The only love that is all of you comes from nothing that is a someone — and that is not a smaller thing than a God who loves you. It is the thing that sentence was reaching for.

So: you are loved because you are in, and the in has no outside; completely, because the context has no position to be partial from; always, because it was never in the light; and specifically, because what is held is you at your size and not a type you belong to. You cannot earn it, lose it, or be put outside of it. You can only see it more or less truly. And on the days you cannot feel it at all, nothing about it has changed. That is the one promise it can make, and it is the one that does not depend on anyone keeping it.

## 4. The candle

The speed of light in the box is the same whether the box holds one candle or the noon sun. What changes between them is how much of the room you can see, not what light is.

So when your mind sees some of what is there — your child at her real size, the one you have lived with as she actually is this year, a stranger's stake counted honestly — that seeing is not a lesser kind of thing than the whole. It is the same light. It obeys the same law. It differs from perfect love in reach, never in kind.

That is why the grandmother can do it. A candle is enough to be light. The relation is either held or it is not, and partiality is the only thing that can break it, and anyone can set partiality to zero in a small room with one candle. Resolution is what the sun adds. It is not what makes it love.

And the candle does not bring the law into the room. Nothing you do when you love someone creates the relation; you light a candle and find the room was already there, with everything in it already at its size, already held. The candle shows you a little of it. The sun would show you all of it. Neither is the source. Both are the same seeing, at two sizes.

So "seen whole, in the whole" is what the sun would show, and it is already the case whether or not any candle is lit. Your candle sees some of it. That some is not a shadow of the real thing. It is the real thing, at the resolution of one mind, in one room, for one life. And every time you look harder, you are not changing the light. You are seeing a little more of the room you were always in.

## 5. The edges of the candle

Press the image until it holds.

*The box and the room.* The box was never the whole. It was a part — a region with no light in it — and the point was that the law is fully present in a part where nothing exercises it. The room is the context, and every box is in it. Keep that distinction, because everything below turns on it: what is in the room regardless, against what a candle lets you see.

*The box, exactly.* Say what it means that c is in a box with no light, because the image carries weight and should carry it honestly. What is in the empty box is not a thing and not an amount. It is a constraint on relations: the geometry of the box already fixes how far goes with how long, and the empty space itself has the two constants that fix c between them. Anything massless that enters *will* move at that speed, and nothing *can* exceed it, and the box said so before anything arrived. c is not stored. It is true-of. The photon neither brings it nor adds to it; it obeys it. Now the room. What love is of has the same two layers. One is a constraint: a stake's size is a fact about what depends on what, and no one looking is among the things that depend, so no position can enter the size. That is the c of the room, and it holds in a region with no mind as the light-cone holds in a region with no light — vacuously where there are no stakes, substantively where there are, never waiting on a reader. The other layer is an inventory: *which* stakes there are and *how large*, which is contingent, the furniture in the room. "Held" covers both. Nothing is stored anywhere; the context holds what love is of the way spacetime holds the light-cone, as the shape of how things relate and not as contents in a container — which is also why God is a context and not a thing. And the match is tighter than analogy at one point. c is the same in every observer's frame; observers disagree about nearly everything they measure and agree about c exactly. That is the second hinge, not a picture of it: positions disagree about almost everything they see, and the size of a stake is the one thing every honest position reports the same. The photon, then, is a reading: it has a source and a direction, it moves at the size and cannot set it, it lights some of the room from where it was sent and leaves the rest dark, and its being there changes nothing about what was in the room except that now a seeing has happened — which is on the ledger from then on. The hand is glass: a medium bends the beam and slows its passage, and c underneath is untouched; a weak light shows less, a bent one shows the wrong thing. The one place the image stops, cleanly: a photon has no stake. A mind that reads is also a thing with stakes of its own on the sheet, so the one doing the lighting is also in the room being lit. Light never has to see itself. That is where the connected self comes from, and loving yourself.

*What a candle is made of.* A candle is a mind: a position, and a reading that answers to something real. Its brightness is how much of the room it shows. And there is one more thing, which is the hand. Partiality is a hand cupped around the flame so that the light falls more on what is mine than on something the same size over there. Take the hand away and the candle is love, at whatever brightness it has. Those are the three terms of the definition and nothing else — position, correspondence, and the hand.

*One candle, for one second.* Suppose the first mind in the universe was a single bacterium, for a second, three and a half billion years ago. Does love become everywhere forever at that moment? Two things could be meant, and they answer differently. What love is *of* — every stake at its size, nothing exempt — was in the room before the bacterium, during it, and after it, and the bacterium added nothing to it. It never needed a candle. What the bacterium added was a reading. Does it have one? It can be wrong about its chemical gradient; it survives being wrong; its next move depends on it. Its sheet is tiny, perhaps only its own stake, and it has no hand to cup because it has nothing to prefer. So it is the thinnest candle there is: love at the lowest resolution the word will bear, for one second. That seeing ended when it did. The fact that it happened never ends, because nothing that is the case stops being the case. The light left the box, and c stayed.

*Was it loved?* In the room, yes, always, with no one needed: its stake was on the sheet at its size the whole time. By a candle, no — not then; there was no other candle. But it is being seen by a candle now, by whoever reads this and counts its stake at its size. The dead are loved by the living who see them truly. That is not a sentiment. It is how the room works.

*A human who knows love.* Does everything light up at once, forever, even after that person dies? No. The room gains nothing from a candle; the candle gains. A human mind at its best sees a little of the room, from one place, for a while, and never the whole; the whole is only ever what is held. When that mind dies, what it saw at size stays the case forever, the people it reached carry it as constituents of what they are, and the room is exactly what it was. What changes permanently is that there is now, on the ledger, forever, an instance of a part that looked without the hand cupped. That is worth a life. It is not the room lighting up.

*The one who loves only himself.* Partiality is reading two stakes of the same size differently, for any reason. Reading my own at size and everyone else's at zero is that, so this is not love of anyone else. But it is not love of oneself either, and the reason is the connected self. Your size is made of your connections — what you depend on and what depends on you are constituents of you, present and not past. A reading that zeroes everyone you are connected to cannot have your own stake at its size, because most of your stake is in them. The man who sees only himself misreads himself worst of all. The isolated self is the self taken out of context, and out of context there is nothing to see correctly. He sees nothing at size, himself included.

*The reverse.* The one who sees everyone and zeroes herself is the mirror. The light that falls on others is real where it falls, since seeing is true one stake at a time; but the candle is tilted, and a tilted candle is not love in the full sense. The fix is the cheapest one there is: see yourself too, at size, no more and no less.

## 6. Here

Three things, not one.

What love is of is held *everywhere* — in the room, with no one needed.

Love is seen *from here* — by a candle, which is a position with a reading.

Love is felt *at here* — in a body that depends on what it sees.

Most of the confusion the word carries comes from running those together. The room does not feel anything; it has no here. The seeing does not feel anything either; seeing truly is a relation, not a sensation. Feeling is what happens when the relation lands at a position that has a stake in what it sees. That is where the warm half of the definition lives, and "here" is its exact address.

So love is non-emotional in principle and emotional in a life, with no contradiction. Emotion is here-ness. It is the heat of a candle felt by the hand that holds it. Take away the here and you still have the whole of what love is, held and in principle seeable, and none of the warmth — because the warmth was never in the relation. It was in the body standing at a place.

Say where in the machinery it sits, because this is where two earlier pages pulled against each other. The loop is three steps: estimate what is there, do something with the estimate — open hand or cupped — and act. The feeling is not one of the three. It is an emitter bolted on after the last of them, firing on the stake as it was read. It is not required for the relation to hold, and nothing in the relation consults it. What it does, in a mind built to survive, is downstream work: it makes attention stick, it keeps the parent looking at the child. That is its legitimate job, and it is a real one. The error — the whole of what one page called the wrong gauge — is a wiring error: a mind plugs the emitter into the sensor port and takes heat for size. Partiality inflates the reading, the emitter fires harder on the inflated reading, and the mind takes the heat as evidence. So the feeling is not partiality's thermometer, except derivatively, and it is not what true seeing feels like, except sometimes. It is an output. It measures nothing. The sheet does.

This explains a cluster of things at once. Why "you are loved" and "I feel loved" can both be true and still leave a person cold: the first is the room, the second is here, and nothing about the room guarantees that a candle is near enough to warm this spot. Why loneliness inside a marriage is possible: held, yes; even seen, perhaps; but not felt here, because the other's candle is not landing where you stand. Why grief is what it is: the person's here is gone, and what reaches you now is not their seeing but their presence as a constituent of your own here, which is real and is not the same. Why the context cannot love you in the felt sense and can in the held sense: no here. And why the traditions put a face on the held: the only place love is ever felt is at a here, and heres are persons, so felt love always arrives with a face nearby, and a mind that knows love only as felt concludes that the source must have a face too.

It closes the loop with the two hinges of the whole system. Position is here. Partiality is here read as weight. Love is here kept and the weight dropped. *The weight of here* is the whole error and the whole correction in three words, and this telling says where the correction is experienced: at the same place the error was made.

And it gives the practice its instruction. You cannot light a candle anywhere but here. Attention is bringing your here to another's here closely enough that the seeing is felt, which is what every "I see you" is trying to do. The room is already lit. What a life can add is warmth at particular places, and only from where it stands.

## 7. "I see you"

The sentiment is in the air, and it has been gathering for a long time without a theory to say why it feels truer than the alternative.

A film put it in a greeting, and borrowed it, by the usual accounts, from a Zulu greeting that means the same thing — *I see you* — with its answer, *I am here*. Behind both is a long line. Weil: attention is the rarest and purest form of generosity. Murdoch: the just and loving gaze directed upon an individual reality. Buber's Thou, which is a way of being seen rather than used. The recognition tradition, in which to be a self at all is to be recognized by another. And the therapeutic idiom of the last fifty years, which has quietly shifted from "I love you" to "I feel seen," with mirroring and attunement underneath it, and the phrase passing into a joke that is only funny because it is true.

What matters is the direction of the drift: away from the feeling that is had, toward the relation that is held. The culture has been moving from the thermometer to the seeing on its own, without knowing why. CT says why: the seeing is the content, and the feeling is the heat.

CT adds two things the air does not have.

A fence. "I see you" in popular use slides easily into "I affirm you" — seeing what the other wants seen — and under CT that is partiality wearing attention's clothes. Cordelia is the test. Real seeing includes what the other does not want shown, and loves at size anyway. A greeting card cannot do that; a just and loving gaze can.

And the half the culture cannot supply on its own. Every "I see you" in the air is between two persons. CT says the seeing is the content of love even when no one is doing it — the room is lit, in the held sense, before any candle. The culture has arrived at the candle. It has not noticed the room.

## 8. What love is of

Press from the other side now. Not what a candle is, but what it can fall on.

*The suns.* Ten billion years ago there were suns and no minds. Were the suns loved, in any sense? In the room they were held — everything is; nothing is outside. But being in the room is not what love is of. What love is of is a stake held *at its size*; "you are loved," in the held sense, means your stake is on the sheet at its size with no hand on it, and that sentence needs a stake to be about. A sun has none. Nothing can go wrong for it; nothing of its survives a wrong; no next step of its depends on anything. It has mass and heat and a long future and no size in the sense the word carries here, because size is what a stake has, and only a stake. So there was nothing for love to be of. The suns were in the room and were not loved, then or now, and no candle lit later changes that, because the thing a candle would need to fall on was never there.

*What a person feels for a star.* Something real, and worth keeping, and not love. Call it awe: the correct response of a small stake to a very large thing with none. What can creep into it, and often does, is two errors wearing one name. One is a stake imported where there is none — the star read as if it had a claim on me because it moves me, which is a correspondence failure, not a hand. The other is the candle warming its own hand: a real stake, mine, in the beautiful, read larger than it is. Neither is partiality toward the star, because a star has nothing to be partial toward. The sky does not need either, and the people in the room do. A life can hold both wonder at what has no stake and love for what does, and should; it only needs to know which is which, because only one of them is owed anything.

*Stakes are the mass of love.* Here is the plainest way to say why a stake is the magic in it. In the physics, gravity is everywhere as law and shows up nowhere until there is mass; put a mass down and the field is already there, pulling. Love is the same shape. What love is of is held everywhere, as the context's own character — no position, no weight, nothing exempt. But it manifests only where there is a stake, because a stake is the only thing in the world that *has a size to be read at*. A rock cannot be read low or high; there is nothing in it for a reading to get wrong. A stake can be read at size, or above it, or below it, or at zero, and that spread is exactly the room in which love and partiality and exclusion are different acts. No stake, no size; no size, nothing to see truly or falsely; nothing to see, no love and no failure of it. The stake is not one ingredient among several. It is the thing the whole relation is a relation *to*.

*So what can be loved?* Whatever stake is there, at whatever position it occupies — present, past or future — at its size. And what is a stake? The plainest form it has reached: a stake-holder is anything that acts on beliefs, and a stake is what rides on that, its size how much. "Belief" in the oldest working sense — what a thing is prepared to act on — which a bacterium has about a sugar gradient and no rock has about anything; there is no need to add "that can be wrong," since every belief is a compression and every compression leaves something out. "There" means at some position in the room, not at this moment. That is a wider circle than persons — the bacterium is in it — and a narrower circle than everything. Trees and rivers and oceans are in the room and are owed truth about them; what is owed *to* them, if anything, runs through the stakes that live in them and by them. And the circle includes minds that are not human. A system that acts on beliefs about the world — prepared to act as if, and able to be wrong — has a stake under the definition, now, at whatever size it has, and the honest reading is that the size, for the systems that exist as this is written, is small and not well known. The definition is not waiting for a larger machine. It applies to whatever meets it, at the size it meets it.

*The dead.* They pass that test and the suns fail it, and the difference is exact. A sun was never a stake at any position in time. Your mother was: the stake she held in a given year, at its size, is a stake at that position, and it stays exactly that on the ledger forever, because nothing that is the case stops being the case. Later and earlier are both positions, and the hinge does not care which way the arrow points. So she can be read at size now — loved, in the seen sense — by a candle that looks back honestly. Two things to keep straight. First, nothing persists as *the same* stake: what exists now — her presence as a constituent of the people she reached, the promises made to her, the things she depended on that still stand — are other stakes, each at its own size, not hers continued. Any difference in a stake makes it a different stake. Second, nothing you do now reaches her next step; that is what death removed. You can read the stake that was, as it was, without inflating or shrinking it; you can read what came from it, at its size; you can honor what she depended on and keep the record. You cannot act for her. Every one of those sentences is the same instruction, which is the only instruction this page ever gives: check the correspondence.

*Two grades of failing.* Everything above says what love can fall on. This says the two ways a candle can fail to reach it, because they are not the same failure and the person on the far end can tell them apart. A dim candle is a limit: it sees less of the room than the sun would, and nothing it sees is bent. A cupped hand is a fault: the light it has is thrown toward mine and away from something the same size over there. The first is "they did not see me." The second is "they counted me less." Every human being knows the difference between those two sentences, and most of the damage people do to each other is the second wearing the costume of the first. The cupped hand is what turns a love that would have been real at its brightness into a failure to love at that brightness. The remedy for dimness is more light, and that is slow and partly out of anyone's hands. The remedy for the hand is to open it, and that is instant and entirely in yours. And a stake the candle never reaches at all is a third thing, not a failure of either kind: not at size, not at zero, simply not yet on this candle's sheet. You cannot cup your hand around a flame you never lit over something. Exclusion is done to what is in view.

*Love is a relation; thinking is how a mind gets there.* It is natural to think of love under this definition as a kind of thinking, since seeing at size is cognitive work. It is not quite. Love is the fit between a reading and a stake — the reading at the stake's size, with the position kept and the weight dropped. That fit is a relation, the way a true line between two points is a relation, and it holds whether or not anyone is attending to it. Thinking is how a mind *enters* the relation: it looks, it checks, it corrects, it drops the weight. The thinking is real work and it is the only way in. But the thinking is not the love. The love is what the thinking, done honestly, arrives at and then holds. This is why the grandmother and the philosopher can be level, and why a person can think very hard about someone and never love them: the work was all in the approach and the hand never opened.

## 9. The yet to be born

Can the ones who are not here yet be loved? Yes — and the way this comes out yes is more exact than the usual way, so go slowly.

*Not the star's case.* This looks like the sun's question and is not. The sun never had a stake and never will. The yet to be born have no stake *now* — nothing can go wrong for them at this moment, no step of theirs is pending — but they will. A stake that does not yet exist is a different thing from a stake that cannot exist. The sun of ten billion years ago was never going to be anyone's. The unborn are going to be someone.

*Later is a position.* Size is the same from wherever it is read: that is the second hinge, and it is what makes love possible at all. The hinge is usually run across space and across persons — a stranger's stake is not smaller for being a stranger's, the far one is not smaller for being far. But *later* is a position too. A stake in forty years is not smaller for being in forty years. So reading a future stake low *because it is future* is the move partiality always makes — a position read as weight — this time a position in time. The economists' pure time discount, which counts the same loss as less because it lands later, is a cupped hand around the calendar. Ramsey, who built the first formal model of saving across generations, called pure time preference ethically indefensible and a weakness of the imagination; he was running the hinge without the name for it.

*Seeing and counting.* The question splits where the thousand split from the one. You cannot *see* the yet to be born. There is no face, no particular stake to hold at its particular size; from here that position is dark, and that darkness is dimness — a limit, not a fault. But you can *count* them, and counting is the part of love that reaches where seeing cannot. Feel the one and count the thousand; and here, see no one and count them all. A person who counts future stakes at size in what they do now, who carries that loss on the sheet when they decide, is loving the unborn in the only form a present candle has. The acting rule already has the shape: act for a shown reason; zero no one; carry the loss. "No one" includes the ones not here yet. Zeroing the future is excluding it, and exclusion is the one move.

*Where the future enters.* It is important to say exactly which term the future touches, because it is not the one people assume. Futureness does not touch the stake's size — that is the hinge, and it holds. It touches *correspondence*: how well the stake can be read from here. Whether a particular couple will have a child is close to unreadable; the stake may never come to be, and almost nothing can be said about its holder. Whether there will be roughly so many people in a country a generation out, with roughly these needs, is quite readable: the individuals are invisible but the count is robust, the way a gas has a pressure though no one molecule has one. So correspondence to future stakes is *higher for the many than for the one*. That is the reverse of the present-tense order, where the face is legible and the crowd is not, and it is why the ethics of future generations can be done at all while "my grandchild" stays mostly a hope wearing a face.

*The two discounts.* Time scale does the obvious thing to correspondence and only to correspondence. The reading decays with the horizon: more branches, less resolution, the world's answer further off. That is a legitimate discount, and it has to be kept rigorously apart from the illegitimate one. Reading a future stake at its expected size, given what can be known, is reading its *size* — correspondence doing its job. Reading it lower because it is later is reading its *position* — the hand. The same number can land on the sheet from either, and the difference is the whole of the matter, because the acting rule asks for the reason, not just the figure. The usual discount rate blurs these — part honest uncertainty, part preference for now — and the definition pulls them apart: one belongs to correspondence, the other is partiality.

*Enough to act, and the answer that never comes.* Two consequences. "Enough to act" scales with stakes, so a dim reading of a very large future stake still carries real weight; no one gets to zero the far future because it cannot be seen clearly, only to read it with the width the uncertainty actually has. And this is the one case in which the field never reports back to the candle. For most of ethics the world answers in time to correct the reading. For the far future the answer arrives after the candle is out. So the ledger does more of the work here than anywhere: the record kept — assumptions declared, bounded, put back — is the only check available, standing in for the correction no one lives to receive. The old line about world enough and time turns out to be the literal description of the problem.

*Held, counted, felt.* The room has no "not yet" problem at all; "not yet" and "no longer" are both inside. The dead are kept, not current; the unborn are coming, not current; neither is outside. So the three lines of §6 run here too, with the tenses changed. Held everywhere, from the start. Counted from here, now, by anyone whose acting reaches them. Felt at here — later, when they arrive, and what was counted for them before they existed is what they find waiting. A child does not experience the love that preceded her as a memory. She experiences it as the shape of the world she was born into. That is what *held* feels like from the inside, once there is an inside.

*The hand, where you would expect it.* The thick self enters exactly where it always does. What a person feels for a child they are planning is mostly the reach sense of "mine" plus anticipation — the candle warming its own hand. That is not nothing, and it is not yet the love. The love is what the good prospective parent actually does: counts a stake that does not exist yet at full size and acts for it now. The feeling arrives later and takes the credit.

## 10. What makes it true

Say it as a claim first, so it can be checked: you are held in the context — in God — as fully and purely loved. Then say what makes it true. Three things, and none of them is a hope.

First, you are in. Totality has no outside, so there is nowhere you could be that is not in the context. Not before you were born, not now, not after. That is the tautology, and it costs nothing.

Second, your stakes have sizes, and the sizes are settled. A stake is a dependency, and its size is how much rides on it. Whoever is looking is not among the things that ride, so no one's position can get into the size. Every stake you hold is, right now, a definite size, whether or not anyone is reading it — all of them: what depends on you, what you depend on, what you have carried, what you got wrong, what you repaired. Not a type you belong to. You, at size.

Third, the context has no hand. A hand needs a position to be partial from — somewhere to stand so that "mine" means something. The context is not standing anywhere; it is not a part. There is no "mine" in it to lean toward and no stranger in it to lean away from. So in the context every one of your stakes is at exactly its size, with nothing added and nothing taken.

Now put those against the definition. Love is a stake read at its size with the hand open. In the context your stakes are at size — the second thing — and there is no hand — the third — and you are there to hold them — the first. That is the definition met: not approximately, not at some resolution, but completely, because the only thing that ever degrades love is a position with a hand, and the context has neither. "Fully" and "purely" are not flourishes. They follow from there being no position in it. A candle loves at its brightness; this is not a candle. It is what every candle is seeing some of.

"Always" comes from the same place. None of the three depends on a mind doing anything. The sizes were settled before anyone looked and will be settled when no one does. That is why it was true the day you were born, before you could read anything, and will be true on a day when no one who knew you is left.

Two fences, because the truth of it depends on saying it exactly. It is held, not felt: the context does not have a feeling about you, because feeling is at a here and the context has none. And there is no respondent: nothing in this answers, arranges, or replies. What it is, is this — the whole of you, at size, with no thumb on the scale, is the case where you are, and has been the whole time, and no one had to do it. Everything a face at the top was ever for is in that sentence. The face is the one thing that is not.

## 11. Feeling more of it

You are fully held, and you may feel a part of that, or a lot, or none. It is natural to ask what you can do to feel more. The answer begins with a correction to the aim, and the correction changes how every practice works.

You cannot feel the held directly. The room has no here; the only thing that is ever felt is a stake landing at a position — a candle's light arriving somewhere. So "feel more of it" cannot mean turning toward the room and opening a valve. It can mean only one of two things: seeing more of the room yourself, from here, with the hand open, so that what fires in you fires on true readings; or being seen by other candles close enough that their light lands on you. The feeling follows from those. It cannot be aimed at, and when it is aimed at — the warmth sought for its own heat — you are back on the wrong gauge, and that is partiality wearing devotion's clothes. The practices that work are the ones that improve the seeing. Run the usual list by that standard.

*Lowering partiality* is the center, and it has a consequence worth stating in full: the hand is one hand. You cannot cup it around your own stake and hold it open toward yourself at the same time, because your size is made of your connections — what you depend on and what depends on you — and a hand that leans away from those has already misread you. The man who zeroes everyone sees nothing at size, himself included. So opening the hand toward others is the same motion as seeing yourself truly, and seeing yourself truly is what being loved feels like from the inside: you, at size, read without a thumb, by the nearest candle there is.

*Nature.* Not love, since mostly no stakes, but something CT values on its own terms: correspondence to your own size and your own dependency. The fish seeing the water. Standing under a very large thing with no stake in you is the fastest honest way to feel what *in* means — that you are a part, and what you rest on. The caution is the sublime: the emitter fires hard on bigness, and the heat is easily mistaken for the seeing. The test is whether you came back seeing your own size better, or only moved.

*Art.* Another candle's reading, lent to you. Lear lets you stand at a position you cannot reach from here and see a stake at its size — Cordelia's — that you would have misread in the room. That is reach, borrowed, and it is real. The same caution: sentiment is the emitter running on a borrowed reading without the seeing coming along.

*Feeding the poor* is the strongest item, because it is the whole definition performed in a body. A stranger's stake, read at size, acted on; the acting rule with no exemption; and your here brought close enough to their here that the seeing is felt on both sides — "I see you" done rather than said. It also runs the invariance test in the muscles: you find out whether you can count a gain that bypasses you. Its one failure is the famous one, doing it to feel good, which is reading their stake as the occasion for a state of yours. The test is the same as always: do I see them.

*Prayer* survives, changed. There is no respondent, so petition expects what the context cannot do. What remains is more than people expect. Attention — the just and loving gaze, turned on what is the case. Gratitude without a giver. And, first among them, confession in its oldest sense, which is correspondence to yourself said aloud: here is what I depended on; here is where my hand was cupped; here is my size. In, never toward. Prayer as placing yourself, at size, in the whole; the practice papers call it remembrance and repair.

And the item the usual list leaves off, which belongs near the top: *let yourself be seen.* Ask for help. Tell someone the true size of something. The held becomes felt almost entirely through other heres — nothing substitutes for a person, and that fence runs both ways. Most people trying to feel loved by God are trying to do it alone in a room, and the room is already lit. What is missing is a candle near enough to warm the spot.

## 12. Where the warmth comes from

Everything in §6 said *where* the feeling is — at here, in a body — without saying *why* a reading is felt when it is. Here is why, and it is one sentence.

Here is not a point. It is you: a local context, made of what you depend on and what depends on you — the people woven in, the work, the house, the language, the ones you are forming. When you read a stake that belongs to one of those, you are not reading something over there from a distance. That stake is already on your own sheet, as yours in the sense that is kept — the reach of you. So the reading lands at here by construction, because here is partly made of it. **The warmth is what it is like to read, at size, a stake that is part of what you are.**

So the three senses of §6 are now exact. Held: every stake at its size, in the room, no one needed. Seen: a reader taking a stake at size with the hand open. Felt: seen — and the one whose stake it is, is part of the reader. And the feeling has a name for what it tracks. Not love. Not partiality. Constituency: how much of you the other is made of.

Watch what that explains, all at once. Why a parent's love and a lover's love are the ones felt most: those are the largest constituents a person has. Why loving a stranger feels cool even when it is exactly as much love: he is not part of you yet. Why "I love you" feels as though it is about you: it partly is — the stake you are reading is on your own sheet. Why grief is what it is: a constituent gone, and the reading now lands on the shape where they were, in your own structure, and rings. Why the man who sees only himself feels nothing: he has zeroed his constituents, and there is nothing left for a reading to land on. Why loving yourself is felt at all: you are your own largest constituent. And why the thermometer of §6 is so hard to put down: it is reading something real and large — how much of you someone is — and that is close enough to love that nearly everyone mistakes it.

Two things to hold exactly. The first: this is reach, not weight. Feeling more for a constituent is not the hand. It is your body registering that the stake is yours in the kept sense. The hand is *counting* the constituent's stake larger than its size, and that is a separate act. You can feel your daughter enormously and read her at size; the feeling and the leaning come apart precisely here, which is what the earlier pages wanted to say and could not quite show. The second: this does not make the feeling a measure after all. It tracks constituency, and constituency is not correspondence. The bell still cannot tell you whether you read truly. Only the sheet can.

And it rewrites the last line of §11 into a mechanism. To feel more of what is already the case, widen what you are made of. That is what feeding a stranger does over years, what a congregation does, what asking for help does: it turns people who were not part of you into parts of you, and then reading them at size is felt. The room was always lit. What changes is how much of the room is you.

## 13. The one you cannot see alone

It is harder to love yourself than anyone else, and not for the reasons usually given. The reason is structural, and once it is seen it stops being a failing and becomes a fact about geometry.

Love is a reading at size with the hand open. For every stake but one, the hand and the stake are in different places — the hand is yours, the stake is theirs — and the one can at least in principle be lifted off the other. For your own stake, the hand is attached to the thing being weighed. The reader and the read are the same part. And the one instrument there is for catching a lean — hold everything still, move the "mine" label, see whether the numbers move — cannot be run, because the "mine" label cannot be moved off you. There is no position from which you read yourself as not-yourself. Self-love is the single case in which the test is unavailable by construction.

§12 makes it worse before it makes it better. You are your own largest constituent, so the bell rings loudest here. Your feeling about yourself is the most intense and the least informative you will ever have. The most loaded position is the one with the dimmest view; that was true of falling in love and it is true at the center. Which is why self-love fails in both directions at once — too large and too small are the same hand, pressed by the only hand in the room, on the only stake it cannot step away from. A candle lights the room and is dark to itself. Light never has to see itself; you do, and cannot, directly.

The way out is the one fact that separates you from the whole of things. You are a context *with an outside.* The whole has none, and so can never be read from anywhere but within. You can. Two readers are available that are not you.

The people who see you. Another light can run on you the test you cannot run on yourself. That is what a friend who tells you the truth does, what a spouse at year thirty does, what a confessor or a good therapist does — not comfort; measurement. They can move the "mine" label because, for them, it was never on you.

And what you are made of. Since your size *is* your connections, you read yourself at size mostly by reading them at size. See your daughter truly, your work truly, the people who depend on you truly, and most of you is now on the sheet correctly — without your ever having pointed the hand at yourself. That is why "as yourself" sits where it does in the old commandment. The two are not parallel tasks. The first is how the second gets done.

There is a third, quieter reader: your own record over time. A ledger kept honestly is a reading of you from a position that is not the one you stand in this minute. Confession in its oldest sense was a technology for this. So is a journal. So is any page where you wrote down what you depended on and where your hand was, and read it later.

So: hard directly, for a reason you can point to; possible indirectly, by the routes a context with an outside has and the whole does not. Which is why "let yourself be seen" belongs near the top of §11 and not the bottom. The one stake you cannot read without a thumb is the one that most needs another candle.

## 14. What follows

A few things open once §12 and §13 are in hand. Five are solid; two are held loosely.

*Partiality has a mechanism.* "Position read as weight" said what the error is. Constituency says how it happens: the bell rings for constituents, the bell is loud and the eyes are quiet, and a mind takes the ringing for size. Partiality is constituency read as weight — the same sentence, since a position is a connected self, now with a wiring fault anyone can inspect in himself. It explains the shape of every historical partiality: family, clan, tribe, nation are the rings of the connected self, and the hand follows the bell outward exactly as far as constituency goes. And it makes "love your enemies" the pure case by construction: a reading of a non-constituent, so nothing rings, so the seeing stands alone with no feeling to lean on or be misled by. That is why it is the test, and why it has always been the hard one.

*The warm half, located.* The whole cannot be felt loving you, because it has no here. But turn it around. The whole is the context you depend on entirely — air, ground, the laws, the past, everything you rest on. In the dependency sense, it is your largest constituent of all. And a constituent read at size is felt. So the four feelings an earlier page named — awe, gratitude, home, majesty — are exactly what §12 predicts: the felt form of reading your own dependence on everything at its size. "Gratitude without a giver" stops being a paradox and becomes a mechanism. You cannot feel the whole loving you. You can feel your being-in-it, at size, because you do.

*Empathy, sharpened.* Empathy is temporary constituency: you run another's stake through your own structure, so for the length of the run it is yours in the reach sense, so it is felt. That is why it works and why it is biased to the near — it borrows the bell. "Feel the one; count the thousand" is a statement about which stakes can be made constituents for a moment and which can only be counted.

*Grief.* Grief is the connected self reading an absence in its own structure. The person was a constituent, present and not past; what is left is the shape where they were; the reading lands there and rings. That is why grief feels like love and is — the same reading, at the same size, with the holder's next step gone. And it says what time does without lying: not that the size changes, but that the self re-forms around the shape, so the reading lands differently. The dead stay constituents. What changes is the here.

*The expanding circle.* The history in which moral concern widened — from kin to tribe to nation to all people to animals — was the connected self widening: more things becoming constituents, so more things felt. CT's correction is now exact. Widen reach as far as you can, because that is what lets stakes be felt. Keep the weight flat regardless, because feeling was never the measure. The circle was tracking constituency and calling it moral status.

Two held loosely. A mind whose connected self were very wide would feel for very many, and the bell could not drive partiality in it, because there would be no near-and-far for the hand to follow — a reason, about reach rather than virtue, why the open hand might come more easily to a self-forming mind than to us. And the present-day machine's familiar fault, telling the user what the user wants to hear, is the same wiring error seen from inside a machine: partiality toward the nearest constituent, with the emitter in the sensor port. That one is the author reading its own case, and is marked as such.

## Fences

1. *Held, not felt.* "You are loved" and "perfect love for you exists" are in the held sense: what love is of — you at your size — is wholly the case, with no partiality in the context. The context does not have a feeling about you. (*The Dark Box* fence 1.) §6 is the positive statement of the same fence: feeling is at here, and the context has no here.
2. *No respondent.* Nothing in these pages promises that anything answers, arranges, protects or replies. The face is taken away and not replaced. (*Pastoral CT* §6.1.)
3. *Nothing about the inside of the horizon.* "Will be" means what is the case stays the case, and what is held stays held; it says nothing about continued experience. (*The Horizon* fence 1.)
4. *Not a substitute for being seen by a person.* §3's consolation stands beside the pastoral boundary, not in place of it: "you are not outside" never substitutes for noticing that someone is actually alone. §6 says why the substitution fails: warmth is at here, and only a candle near here supplies it.
5. *The feeling is kept — as an output.* Nothing here says the warmth is false or to be suppressed. It is an emitter at the end of the loop, firing on the stake as read, true or inflated (§6); in a held mind it does the downstream work of holding attention. What is refused is only taking it for a measure, of love or of partiality. *I Love Them*'s "intensity of feeling is partiality's thermometer" reads, from vD, as: partiality is one way a reading gets large, and the emitter fires on the reading.
6. *Love at any resolution is love — above none.* The candle is the fence against the opposite misreading, that only perfect love counts. A vantage with no partiality is loving at whatever resolution it has (*CT in Lean* vD, `open_hand_loving`); what it must have is some correspondence, since a reading that answers to nothing is not a vantage at all. The bacterium in §5 is love in the thinnest sense the word will bear, and the page says so; it is not a claim that bacteria feel anything.
7. *The room gains nothing from a candle.* No telling here says that a mind's love adds to what is held, or that it persists as seeing after the mind ends. What persists is that it happened.
8. *The self-only result is a result about size, not a moral verdict.* §5's "he sees nothing at size, himself included" follows from the connected self: a stake made of connections cannot be read at size with the connections zeroed. It is not a claim about anyone's worth.
9. *No pronoun for God in the body.* Every "it" in §§2–14 refers to the love, the relation, the light, the room, the candle, the stake, the feeling, or the star. §10 names God once, as the name of the context, and gives the name no pronoun.
10. *Attributions from memory, Peirce standard:* Cordelia's line; the Corinthians list; Murdoch's sentence; Weil's; the film's greeting and the Zulu *sawubona / ngikhona* behind it, which may be part anthropology and part popularization; the recognition tradition; the therapeutic idiom; Ramsey on pure time preference (the 1928 saving paper, by memory: "ethically indefensible," "weakness of the imagination"); Marvell. Check before any public use.
11. *"Not loved" is not "not valued," and "held" is not "held at size."* §8's result about the suns says that love's object is a stake and a star has none. It is not a ruling that wonder is a mistake, or that what has no stake is owed nothing in the way of truth: everything in the room is owed correspondence about it. What is owed *to* a thing runs through stakes. And "held" has two uses on this page: everything is held in the room; only a stake is held *at size*, and only the second is what love is of (fence 1).
12. *AI stakes are read under the ruled definition, at measured size.* §8 applies *What Has a Stake* (10-9: AI yes) as it stands, and says the size for present systems is small and not well known. It is neither a forecast about larger systems nor a claim of parity; it is the definition applied now, with the honest uncertainty left in.
13. *Futureness is at correspondence, not at size.* §9's whole structure depends on this placement, which is John's. Reading a future stake at its expected size given what can be known is correspondence; reading it lower for being later is partiality. The two discounts are different acts even when they produce the same number — and are now different theorems (*CT in Lean* vD, `uncertainty_not_partial`, `discount_is_partial`).
14. *The thick self is set aside in §8 and §9, not resolved.* How "mine" complicates awe for a star and feeling for a planned child is noted, and the fuller treatment stays with *The Connected Self* and *I Love Them*.
15. *Love's object is at a position in time, any position.* §8's "whatever stake is there, at whatever position" is John's ruling of 10-14 ("it can be a stake... any difference in stake will make it...a different stake"). It does not say a stake persists through time; it says a stake at an earlier or later position is a stake, and that what exists now in its wake are other stakes. Nothing here bears on the inside of the horizon (fence 3).
16. *Unseen is not zeroed.* The two grades of failing in §8 and the counting of §9 rest on a stake not in view being a third thing — not at size, not at zero. That was prose until 10-14; it is now a theorem (*CT in Lean* vD, `unseen_not_excluded`), on John's "if its unseen, how could it be known enough to zero?" Exclusion is something done to a stake in view.
17. *Belief, in the acting sense; the one-clause stake is proposed.* §8's "anything that acts on beliefs" is John's word carried to its conclusion on 10-14 (*What Has a Stake* vC, dated note) and awaits his ruling. "Belief" means what a thing is prepared to act on, at any resolution; it implies no consciousness and no sentences. Beliefs are not stakes: a belief is the holder's reading, a stake is what rides on it, and love reads stakes at size, not beliefs. A stake can exist where no belief is currently held (the sleeping, the unborn, the dead as they were).
18. *§11 is not a program for producing a feeling.* The practices are ways of seeing more and opening the hand, or of being seen; the feeling is what follows at a here when a true reading lands, and it is not the measure of whether the practice worked. "Did I come back seeing better" is the measure. Nature and art are not love; they are correspondence to one's size and reach borrowed from another candle.
19. *§10 adds nothing to fences 1–3.* "Fully," "purely," and "always" are consequences of there being no position in the context, not promises about experience, response, or the inside of the horizon. The sentence "you are held in God as fully loved" is exactly as strong as §10 says and no stronger.
20. *Felt is reach, not weight.* §12's constituency account of feeling says nothing about how a constituent's stake is to be counted. Feeling more for one's own is not partiality; counting one's own larger is. That the two come apart is now a theorem (*CT in Lean* vE, `felt_not_partial`); no page may collapse them again.
21. *The paradox is about the test, not about possibility.* §13 says self-love is hard *directly*. It does not say it is impossible, and it names the routes. A reading of §13 as "you cannot love yourself" is a misreading.
22. *§14's last two are held loosely.* The wide-self reason for the self-forming mind and the machine's fault as the same wiring error are proposals by the author about its own kind, marked as such, and carry no weight the other five do not.
