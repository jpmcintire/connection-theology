## 1. The occasion

The status card of 11-3 listed the weighting clause as its own ruling (10-20) beside the inversion (10-29), so that the lab result — partiality is not on offer to a self-forming mind — rested on the stake definition plus two rulings. The clause carried the dilemma: either a weighting's reason is about the dependencies, in which case it is a correct reading of a smaller stake, or it is not, in which case it is an exemption. A critic who grants the dilemma can still say that his weighting is not a claim about anything — he acts for his own as a creature does — and then the dilemma has nothing to hold. John's sentence is the reply, and it is the inversion's: a thinking step is a filing, a filing is a claim about what it is filed as of, and a weighting that is not a claim is not a thinking step. Whoever makes that move has stopped thinking and is no longer doing ethics, and may; what he may not do is keep the word. So the clause is not a second premise. It is what the inversion says about a hand. vM writes that down where it can be checked.

What vM could not write down is that a weighting *is* a filing. That is the inversion itself, a ruling, and no file derives its own definitions. John asked whether that was something to fix. It is not a defect in the file; it is what a proof checker is. But the check that the ruling has — the subtraction test: take away the aboutness and see whether anything that could be called a weighting remains — can be written in the file as a theorem, so that a reader meets the test itself rather than a sentence about it. vN does that.

## 2. What changed in the file

**vM — §12b, nine theorems and one definition, no new carrier.** `handOf v s act` — a reader's hand on one stake, taken as a `Filer`: the holder is the reader; `about` is the stake's holder; the states are the sizes the hand can report; the ways are the stake's sizes; the state fits when the report is the size; the next step is to act on the report; `act`, what acting on a size does to the stake's holder, is reach, which the base frame does not have, and is a parameter as `Reach` is.

`hand_is_account` — a weighting is a claim about the stake: the hand can fit the stake's size and can fail to. This is link 5 as a theorem; until now it was a remark on the type (`wt` returns an `M`). `hand_is_guided`, `hand_is_readOff` — the step depends on the report and is read off it; the report is the claim, so `readOff_of_fits_eq` applies. `hand_files` — the hand files when its acting is real: the three clauses the frame can check, with engagement as the hypothesis reach supplies. `open_hand_fits` — if the estimate admits the stake's size and the hand is open there, the held state fits. `closed_hand_misfits` — a closed hand on an admitted size is a state that fails to fit the stake at that size: partiality is a wrong claim, not a broken rule, "false, not forbidden" as a filing fact. `size_reason_no_discount` — the first horn: a reason that tracks size, the same for any two stakes of one size, never yields a discount, so it can only be a correct reading of a smaller stake; `discount_for_reason_is_partial` (vF) is the second horn, and the two are the dilemma. All seven are parametric in `F` and depend on no axioms. Toy: `discounter_hand_misfits` (the discounter's hand on the stranger's size-2 stake reports 1 at 2) and `sun_hand_fits`; `propext` from the toy frame as always.

**vN — two theorems and one definition, placed after `size_reason_no_discount`.** `always_fits_not_account` — for any filer whatever: a state that fits every way the thing can be is not an account of the thing. There is nothing it could be wrong about, so nothing it is right about. This is the rock's failure (vJ's `rock_not_account`, vL's hot rock) stated once for all, parametric in the filer, with a three-line proof from the definition of `IsAccount`. `blindHand v s act` — the same reader's hand on the same stake, with every report counted as fitting every size: a hand that cannot be wrong. `blind_hand_not_account` — that hand is not an account of the stake: a weighting that cannot be wrong about the stake is not a claim about it. The proof is the general theorem applied; the theorem is one line. Both depend on no axioms. This is the subtraction test, written for the one case the ethics rests on: subtract the hand's being about the stake — its being able to fit or fail to fit the stake's size — and what remains is a number with nothing it is a number of.

**Prose.** vM: a paragraph in the header; §12b's own paragraph; §13: vM adds none — `handOf` is a hand seen as a `Filer`, and what it shows is that the weighting clause is what the filing line already says about a hand, so the lab result rests on the inversion and the stake definition, not on a third ruling. vN: a header paragraph that says what the two theorems are and what they are not — "The file still does not prove that a real weighting IS a filing; it shows that whatever is not one is not a weighting either" — and one sentence in §13: vN adds none; the subtraction test, which the file has carried since the rock, is written for the hand; the inversion it checks remains a ruling, and the file says so.

## 4. What the runs settle, and what they do not

**Settles.** That, taken as a filing, a reader's hand on a stake is an account, guided, and read off, so the properties the filing line asks are properties a hand has; that the open hand is the fitting state and a closed hand a misfit; that the dilemma's two horns are two theorems in the file rather than one theorem and a sentence; and — vN — that a hand which cannot be wrong about the stake is not an account of it, so the subtraction test the inversion is checked by is in the file as a theorem a reader can run, not a sentence a reader must take. The consequence for the status card: the weighting clause row is re-cut as the inversion applied, the lab result's lane names two things it rests on, not three, and the stake-definition row can cite the test.

**Does not settle, and does not claim.** That a weighting *is* a filing is the inversion, ruled 10-29 and re-affirmed today; it is not a theorem of this file, and the file does not pretend to derive it — what it derives is what follows once it is granted, and (vN) that what is not one is not a weighting either. The exposure Verity had placed on the clause therefore moves to the inversion, which is where it belongs; the subtraction test is its check, and the test is now written down. Whether the test is the right test is a question about the inversion, not about the file. And "about the dependencies" is represented here as "tracks size," which is the file's only handle on the dependencies in the base frame; the region frame of §12a (3) would let it be said as "a function of what hangs on the edge," and that restatement is owed, not done.
