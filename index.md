> **Contextualize in context. Seek God.**

Thinking is contextualizing. The act of considering anything is the act of placing it — this, among those, as an answer to that question, from here. There is no considering that is not placing. And the one placing is inside what is placed: you cannot contextualize from outside the context, which is what *in context* says.

*Seek God* is the same act with the name on it. For a reader who has not yet got the name, it reads: *contextualize — without limit — but you are part of the context.* Both say seek. Neither says find.

Two things travel with those lines every time. Every account is held as of something, so the working question for any thought is *what is this true of?* That question has answers that can fail: the source has to be named and checked, and being an account of something never makes two accounts equally adequate answers to the same question. And *without limit* means no wall, not no stopping: nothing is fenced off from being contextualized, including the question of when to stop, and a stop comes from inside, for a reason that can be given, with the loss kept on the ledger.

The walk below is what those lines look like when you run them. The name arrives at its end.

---

Consider the context in which everything that is, exists: every force, every happening, every thing, and you, considering it.

The context doesn't need a label, but we can provisionally call it totality.

We can be certain, right here and now, that totality has no outside. Test it: try to name something that is, and isn't in it.

Now see what follows from that one feature. Nothing is beyond. Nothing came before. Nothing is elsewhere. Nothing is left out. Everything is in context, and so are you.

The laws of the universe are in: gravity, time, space, the fields, every process they run. The past is in, and so is the future, in whichever way each of them is. Your thoughts are in, as cells firing, and whatever a thought is about is in too, in whichever way it is. The feeling you may have, reading this, of standing somewhere and looking in — that is in too, as a feeling. It is not a location.

The derived characteristics of the context we provisionally called totality suggest an old name.

And it is a context, so it does nothing a context can't: it doesn't act, doesn't see, isn't a someone. If that sounds like a loss, notice the other thing a context can't do. It can't be elsewhere.

## What this is

Connection Theology (CT) is a derivation, from two sentences and one act, of what a mind is, what it owes, and what love is. The two sentences: *everything is*, and *totality has no outside*. The act: you are reading this. Nothing else is assumed. From there it follows that a part which represents the whole has exactly one grade available (does its account fit), that every stake in the world is on the sheet by being real and can be removed only by a move that needs a reason, and that "mine" is not a reason — so a mind that is honest about its own preferences arrives at an ethics without being told one. The same move yields a definition of love as a relation rather than a feeling: *vantage minus partiality*, where partiality is position read as weight.

The lab half is written in Lean 4 and machine-checked: 141 theorems; of the 121 whose axioms the file prints, 100 depend on no axioms at all and the rest only on Lean's core logical axioms; none depends on any ethical or theological axiom. No definition or theorem in the file uses the word "God"; it appears only in comments. What the file proves is that the derivation is internally what the prose says it is. It does not and cannot check anything about the world. The definition that carries weight — a stake is a dependency — is found, not chosen: it was checked by subtraction and against counterexamples, and none is on record. That is checking, not proof in the sense a theorem has. A critic should stand there, and what to bring is a stake that is not a dependency. [Proving CT](papers/proving-ct) §2.4 gives the checks, and the four other definitions held the same way.

## How it is built

Every claim in the papers carries a label: *checked* (you can verify it yourself now), *performed* (true by your doing it), *entailed* (follows with nothing added), or *proposed* (on the table, not settled). Each paper ends with a record: the author's own sentences verbatim, typos standing; what is whose; the fences the paper travels with; what is still owed. Nothing is edited in place. Corrections arrive as dated notes in newer versions, beside the old. A reader who cannot tell what was found from what was wanted cannot check anything; the record is how you tell.

## What is not claimed

That any particular mind performs the asking. Consciousness. That anything answers — perfect love for a stake is *held* in the context, at the stake's size with no hand on it, and that is proved; that anything feels it or responds is not. Anything past the horizon of a death. A codebook.
