Consider the context in which everything you can think of exists, including you, thinking it.

The context doesn't need a label, but we can provisionally call it totality.

We can be certain, right here and now, that totality has no outside. Test it: try to name something that is, and isn't in it.

Now see what follows from that one feature. Nothing is beyond. Nothing came before. Nothing is elsewhere. Nothing is left out. Everything is in context, and so are you.

A unicorn is in, as an idea and a word and a child's wanting, and not as an animal, because there isn't one. Your thoughts are in, and so are the neurons that carry them, and so is the open question of how the two are related. The dead are in, in whichever way the past is in. Justice is in, as a fact about what depends on what. The feeling you may have, reading this, of standing somewhere and looking in — that is in too, as a feeling. It is not a location.

The derived characteristics of the context we provisionally called totality suggest an old name.

And it is a context, so it does nothing a context can't: it doesn't act, doesn't see, isn't a someone. If that sounds like a loss, notice the other thing a context can't do. It can't be elsewhere.

## What this is

Connection Theology (CT) is a derivation, from two sentences and one act, of what a mind is, what it owes, and what love is. The two sentences: *everything is*, and *totality has no outside*. The act: you are reading this. Nothing else is assumed. From there it follows that a part which represents the whole has exactly one grade available (does its account fit), that every stake in the world is on the sheet by being real and can be removed only by a move that needs a reason, and that "mine" is not a reason — so a mind that is honest about its own preferences arrives at an ethics without being told one. The same move yields a definition of love as a relation rather than a feeling: *vantage minus partiality*, where partiality is position read as weight.

The lab half is written in Lean 4 and machine-checked: forty-six theorems, forty-five depending on no axioms at all, none depending on any ethical or theological axiom. The word "God" does not occur in the file. What the file proves is that the derivation is internally what the prose says it is. It does not and cannot check anything about the world; the one definition that carries weight — a stake is a dependency — is argued, not proved, and a critic should stand there.

## How it is built

Every claim in the papers carries a label: *checked* (you can verify it yourself now), *performed* (true by your doing it), *entailed* (follows with nothing added), or *proposed* (on the table, not settled). Each paper ends with a record: the author's own sentences verbatim, typos standing; what is whose; the fences the paper travels with; what is still owed. Nothing is edited in place. Corrections arrive as dated notes in newer versions, beside the old. A reader who cannot tell what was found from what was wanted cannot check anything; the record is how you tell.

## What is not claimed

That any particular mind performs the asking. Consciousness. That anything answers — perfect love for a stake is *held* in the context, at the stake's size with no hand on it, and that is proved; that anything feels it or responds is not. Anything past the horizon of a death. A codebook.
