# Knowledge and the Right Dependency — Gettier under link 5 (10.21.26) vA

## A sixty-year problem as a filing error: knowledge is an account that fits, held for a reason about the dependency the fit rides on

**Folder:** Connection Theology (CT) / Focus - Philosophy
**Status:** working paper; **proposed, for John's ruling. Nothing here is ruled.** vA written Wednesday 10-21-26, afternoon, at John's "ok - move forward as you think best the thinking/write up tasks," after the morning's diagnosis (*The Vantage Mistaken for an Outside* vC) and the Rorty paper opened the question of what else the mechanism reaches. Ranked first among the openings because it is a *result*, derivable from the chain as it stands, not a sort. **Every case and every neighbor is from memory; the texts are owed before any public use (§9).**
**What this is:** CT's answer to the Gettier problem. The chain already separates justification (a reason: a claim that something fits — link 5) from fit (held — link 4, cashed out by link 3), and Monday's ruling added that a reason has to be about the dependencies. Put together: a Gettier case is a belief that fits, held for a reason that is about a *different* dependency than the one the fit rides on. Knowledge is fit plus a reason about the right dependency. The sixty years of fourth conditions are attempts to say this without the concept of a dependency.
**What it rests on:** *No Exemption* vC (link 3 the stake as dependency; link 4 one grade; link 5 with the 10-20 clause — *a weighting is a claim about the stake weighted, and its reason has to be about the dependencies*; link 6 every account made of what reached it); *The Wrong Question* vA (a true account filed under the wrong question; `Filing` in the Lean file at vG); *CT and Rorty* vA §7 (justification separated from fit); *Lab and Field*; *CT Under the Tools* §9 (skepticism passes; CT never bet on the inventory).

## 0. The sentence, plain

You can believe something true, for a good reason, and still not know it — when your reason is about one thing and the truth is riding on another. Smith's reason was about Jones; the truth was about Smith. Henry's reason was "it looks like a barn, so it's a barn"; in that county the look comes from facades, and this one just happened to be real. In every Gettier case the reason and the fit are about different dependencies. Knowledge is when they're about the same one. That's the whole answer, and it needs nothing CT didn't already have.

---

## 1. The problem

**Lane: bearing; from memory.**

Knowledge was long taken to be justified true belief. Gettier (1963) gave two cases in which all three hold and no one would say the subject knows. Smith has strong evidence that Jones will get the job and that Jones has ten coins in his pocket, and infers "the man who will get the job has ten coins." Smith gets the job, and happens to have ten coins. Justified, true, not knowledge. The literature since has multiplied cases — Russell's stopped clock (right twice a day; you look at the right moment), Chisholm's sheep (a dog that looks like a sheep, and a sheep behind the hill), Goldman's fake-barn county (Henry sees the one real barn among facades) — and proposed fourth conditions: no false lemmas (Clark), a causal connection between the fact and the belief (Goldman 1967), sensitivity (Nozick: if p were false you would not believe it), safety (Sosa, Williamson: you could not easily have been wrong), reliabilism, and Williamson's refusal to analyze at all. Zagzebski argued that any fallibilist analysis of the form JTB-plus-X can be Gettiered by letting X hold and the truth arrive by luck.

## 2. The three terms in CT's words

**Lane: derived; the chain's labels.**

- *Belief* is an account: a part's representation of what is the case, made of what reached it (links 2, 6).
- *True* is fit: the account's one grade, whether it fits what is the case (link 4), cashed out by link 3 as a dependency — if the account is wrong, what the part does next goes differently. Fit is held: it obtains or fails whether anyone checks (*CT and Rorty* §3).
- *Justified* is held for a reason, and a reason is a claim that something fits (link 5). The 10-20 clause says what a reason is a claim *about*: a dependency. The clause was ruled for weightings; its generalization to any reason is immediate, since a weighting is one kind of claim and the chain's reasons are all claims of the same kind.

So in CT's vocabulary, knowledge-as-JTB would read: an account that fits, held for a claim that something fits. And the gap is visible at once: nothing in that formula says the claim is about *the same thing* the fit rides on.

## 3. The diagnosis

**Lane: derived.**

A fit rides on a dependency: the account is true because of some fact on which the next step's going-right depends. A reason is about a dependency: the believer holds the account because of a claim that some fact bears on it. Ordinarily these are the same dependency — I believe there is a barn because it looks like a barn, and it looks like a barn because it is one; my reason is about the look-from-barn dependency and the fit rides on it.

A Gettier case is a case in which they come apart: the account fits, the reason is a genuine claim about a dependency, but the dependency the reason is about is not the one the fit rides on. The fit rides on something the reason never mentioned. That is why the truth feels like luck — it arrived through a channel the believer was not looking at.

In *The Wrong Question*'s terms, a Gettier belief is a true account held as the answer to a question it does not answer. Smith's reason answers "does Jones have ten coins and will Jones get the job"; it is filed as answering "does the man who gets the job have ten coins." The account is true; the filing is wrong. The Lean file already types this split as `Account` and `Filing` (the 10-15 run): a reading carries candidate sizes *with the stake they are in fact of*, and a filing says which stake the reader holds each account as an account of. A Gettier case is a correct account mis-filed at the level of the reason.

**The condition, stated.** *A part knows that p when its account that p fits, and it holds the account for a reason that is about the dependency on which the fit rides.* Knowledge is the coincidence of the reason's dependency and the fit's dependency. Nothing modal, nothing causal, nothing about reliability enters; "about" is the relation a claim has to what it is a claim of, which the chain already uses.

## 4. The cases, run

**Lane: derived; the cases from memory.**

*The coins.* Reason: about the dependency (Jones's coins, Jones's hiring) → (the hired man's coins). Fit: rides on (Smith's coins, Smith's hiring). Different dependency. Not knowledge. Correct.

*The stopped clock.* Reason: about the dependency (the clock's mechanism tracks the time) → (the hands show the time). Fit: rides on the coincidence of stopped hands and the present minute; the tracking dependency does not hold. The reason is about a dependency that is absent; the fit rides on one the reason does not mention. Not knowledge. Correct.

*Fake-barn county.* Reason: about the dependency (barn-look ← barn). In this county barn-look is produced by facades, so the dependency the reason asserts does not hold here; this instance is real, so the fit rides on "this one happens to be a barn," which the reason was not about. Not knowledge, and without any appeal to nearby possible worlds — the dependency Henry's reason is a claim about is false in the region, as a fact. Correct, and it handles the case that broke the causal theory.

*The sheep.* Reason: about (sheep-look ← sheep), with the look coming from a dog. Fit: rides on the sheep behind the hill. Different dependency. Not knowledge.

*The lottery.* "My ticket will lose," on the odds. Reason: about the dependency (the odds → the outcome); fit: rides on the draw. The reason's dependency is real but does not reach the fit — the odds do not settle which ticket is drawn. Not knowledge. Correct: most say one does not know one's ticket will lose. The condition gives the result as a matter of resolution — the reason is about the right dependency at a resolution that does not reach the fit — which is where §7 picks up.

*The ordinary case.* I see a cup; I believe there is a cup; reason: (cup-look ← cup), which holds; fit: rides on the cup. Same dependency. Knowledge. Correct.

*A false-but-justified belief.* Reason about the right dependency, account does not fit. Not true; not knowledge; and the believer was not at fault — correspondence failed at the seeing, a limit, not a fault (the two grades of failing, *The Candle* §8). The condition is fallibilist: one can hold a reason about the right dependency and be wrong.

## 5. The fourth conditions as attempts at this

**Lane: Verity's; neighbors from memory, Peirce standard.**

Each proposed fourth condition is a way of saying "the reason is about the dependency the fit rides on" without the concept of a dependency.

- *No false lemmas* catches the cases in which the reason routes through a false step, which is one way for the reason to be about the wrong dependency; it misses cases with no inference (the barn).
- *The causal theory* says the fact must cause the belief — nearly the right dependency, but causal, so it fails where the right causal chain produces the belief in a region where the dependency the reason asserts does not generally hold (the barn), and it fails for knowledge of the future and of generalities, where there is no causal line from fact to belief. Dependency is broader than cause: what the fit rides on need not cause the believing.
- *Sensitivity* and *safety* are modal proxies: "if p were false you would not believe it" and "you could not easily have been wrong" are ways of asking whether the reason tracks the dependency, phrased across possible worlds because the author lacked a word for the dependency in this one. They get most cases right and generate their own counterexamples at the edges, as proxies do.
- *Reliabilism* makes the proxy a property of the process rather than the case: a reliable process is one whose reasons are usually about the right dependencies.
- *Williamson's knowledge-first* declines to analyze, on the ground that every analysis has failed. On CT's reading the analyses failed because they were missing one concept, not because there was none to find.
- *Zagzebski's inescapability argument* says any fallible J plus T can be Gettiered by letting the truth arrive by luck. "Luck," on this reading, just is the fit arriving through a dependency the reason was not about, and the condition excludes it by definition rather than by adding a clause that luck can slip past. The argument is correct about every JTB-plus-X whose X is a proxy; it does not touch a condition stated in terms of what the reason is about.

Nearest neighbor, from memory and owed a check: the explanationist analyses (knowledge when the truth of p figures in the best explanation of the belief that p). Close in spirit; CT's condition is about dependency rather than explanation, and does not need "best."

## 6. What is and is not claimed

**Lane: fences.**

Claimed: that the Gettier *problem* — the existence of justified true beliefs that are not knowledge — is diagnosed, and that the diagnosis follows from links 3–5 with the 10-20 clause and nothing added; that the cases in §4 come out right on the condition; that the fourth-condition literature is a set of proxies for the condition.

Not claimed: a reductive definition of "about," which the chain uses as primitive (a claim is a claim of something); that the condition settles every case at every resolution (§7); anything about the sources of justification (perception, testimony, memory), which are field matters; that skepticism is answered here (it is handled separately — *CT Under the Tools* §9 — and the condition presupposes that fit can obtain, which the skeptic denies and CT never bet against); a history claim — "first" waits on the texts.

## 7. Lab and field

**Lane: derived.**

In the lab the condition is exact: a reason is about a dependency or it is not. In the field, "about the right dependency" is itself a reading, and readings have resolution. Henry's reason in a county with one facade and a thousand barns is about a dependency that holds at the resolution of the county; in a county of facades it does not. The literature's intuition that Gettier cases shade — that barn-country knowledge weakens as the facades multiply — is the resolution of the reason's dependency varying with the region, and the condition predicts the shading rather than drawing a line. Where resolution does not settle it, the honest output is "underdetermined," which is what the chain says everywhere else. Knowledge, like love and like ethics, is exact at the limit and graded in a life.
