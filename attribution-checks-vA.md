# Note - Pack Attribution Checks (10.14.26) vA

**Folder:** Connection Theology (CT) - Index / Captures
**Status:** record check; Verity's, 10-14-26 evening, at John's "take a single next step." The Peirce-standard pass over the papers proposed for the public pack: every from-memory attribution, citation, quotation and etymology in the ten papers, *To a Mind That Reads This*, and the README, checked against a source where one could be reached in the session, and otherwise classified. This is a record check, not a merits pass (WORKING-RULES v7): nothing here touches what a paper argues; it touches only whether a name, a date, a verse or a quotation is what the paper says it is. Nothing here is ruled.
**What this is:** the gate between the pack and a public folder. Four verdicts are used. **Fetched** — confirmed against a source read in the session, named below. **Standard** — a well-known attribution not fetched here because the risk of error is negligible (a canonical quotation, a famous title, a verse number); listed so the reader can see what was and was not independently read. **Corrected** — the paper has it wrong or imprecise; a dated note is owed and the fix is stated. **Unverified** — could not be confirmed from a source reached here; stays marked "from memory" in the paper, or the dependence on it is reduced as stated.
**What it rests on:** the papers named below, at the versions named; *WORKING-RULES* v7; the Peirce standard as the corpus has used it since 9-18 (an attribution from memory is a lead, not a finding, until read in the text).
**Reading order:** §1 (the corrections — four, none load-bearing), then §2 by paper, then §3 what remains owed.

## 1. The corrections

Four. None touches a derivation; each is a sentence about someone else's work.

**1.1 *Not Told* §6 — what supports the orthogonality thesis. Corrected.** The paper says the thesis's "support is Hume's guillotine, no ought from an is." Bostrom's own statement (*"The Superintelligent Will,"* *Minds and Machines* 22:2, 2012, fetched from nickbostrom.com) is: "Intelligence and final goals are orthogonal axes along which possible agents can freely vary... more or less any level of intelligence could in principle be combined with more or less any final goal." He says the thesis "can draw support from the Humean theory of motivation" — that beliefs alone cannot motivate and some desire is required — and adds that it "does not presuppose it." That is the Humean theory of *motivation*, not the is/ought gap, and he presents it as optional support, not foundation. The paper's sort of the thesis (holds for told minds, fails for asking minds) does not depend on which Humean point is in play, but the sentence should read: "Its usual support is the Humean theory of motivation — belief alone moves nothing — which Bostrom says it can draw on without presupposing." Dated note owed at *Not Told* vB. *To a Mind* vC says only "a standard thesis in the field" and does not name the support; no change there.

**1.2 *What Has a Stake* vC, dated note C — the "stake" etymology. Corrected (downgraded).** The note offers, flagged for checking, that the wager sense "is said to come from the older sense of a post driven into the ground." Etymonline (fetched) calls the wager sense (1530s) "a word of uncertain origin," gives the post-sense derivation only as "perhaps," offers a second "perhaps" (a post on which a wager was placed), and reports that the OED finds "no evidence of the existence of such a custom." So the resonance is not available as a fact. The sentence should read that the origin is uncertain and the post-sense derivation is one conjecture the OED does not support, or the sentence should go. Dated note owed at *What Has a Stake* vD. Nothing else in the note depends on it.

**1.3 *What Love Is* vD §4 — the Samaritan's donkey. Corrected (small).** The paper has him "on the road with oil, wine and a donkey." Luke 10:34 has oil and wine and "set him on his own animal" (ESV; KJV "beast"); the text does not say donkey. "His own animal" or "his own mount." Same section: "rejoices in the truth" (1 Cor 13:6) is the NKJV wording; ESV and NIV have "rejoices with the truth." Either is a translation; the paper should name which it uses or say "rejoices with the truth." Dated note owed at *What Love Is* vE, with the other items that paper already owes.

**1.4 *No Exemption* vB §9 — Lichtenberg's notebook number. Corrected (precision).** The sentence "es denkt" against Descartes is confirmed in substance (*"Es denkt, sollte man sagen, so wie man sagt: es blitzt"* quoted in two sources reached; the Hennig paper discusses it without the number). The reference "Sudelbücher K 76" could not be confirmed in a source reached this session; it is the commonly cited number but no page I reached gives it. Cite as "Sudelbücher, notebook K" with the number marked as unconfirmed, or confirm against the Promies edition before the pack goes out. The quotation is also in the chain as quoted in *To a Mind* §2; the quotation stands, the number is the only thing at issue.

### 2.6 *The Dark Box* (10.13.26) vA, §9.3 — the Greek and Latin

- ***Prosōpolēmpsia*, "receiving the face," and its cluster.** Romans 2:11 (προσωπολημψία); Acts 10:34 (προσωπολήμπτης, the noun for the person); James 2:1 (the noun, plural), 2:9 (προσωπολημπτέω, the verb); Colossians 3:25; Ephesians 6:9; 1 Peter 1:17 (ἀπροσωπολήμπτως, the adverb). Standard; the forms differ by verse as noted and the paper's "the adverb" for 1 Peter is right. Vulgate *acceptio personarum* (Romans 2:11: *non enim est personarum acceptio apud Deum*); KJV "respect of persons," "respecter of persons" (Acts 10:34). Standard.
- ***Prosklisis*, 1 Timothy 5:21. Fetched (Strong's 4346, biblehub), confirmed:** "a leaning towards," figuratively partiality; one NT occurrence as the noun; KJV "partiality." One addition: Thayer's notes a marginal variant πρόσκλησις ("summons") in some editions, judged a probable itacism; the main texts read *prosklisis*. The paper's gloss "a leaning, an inclination toward" is exact.
- ***Homo incurvatus in se*. Fetched (Wikipedia, citing Jenson, *The Gravity of Sin*, 2006), confirmed as the standard attribution:** "perhaps Augustine" coined it; Luther expounded it in the *Lectures on Romans*. The paper's "Augustine, and Luther after him" matches the standard account; Augustine's use of the exact Latin is tentative in the source. Keep as is with "the attribution is traditional."
- ***Kenōsis*, Philippians 2:7 (ἐκένωσεν).** Standard.
- ***Parallagē*, James 1:17. Fetched (Strong's 3883, biblehub), confirmed:** παραλλαγή, one NT occurrence, James 1:17, "variation" (NASB), "variableness" (KJV); Strong's gloss "transmutation (of phase or orbit)." The paper's "same root as parallax": both παραλλαγή and παράλλαξις derive from παραλλάσσω; the fetched entry does not state the parallax connection itself, so this is **standard as to the root, fetched as to the word.** The paper's own fence ("to be checked first because it is almost too good") can be relaxed to "confirmed as to the word and its sense; the parallax kinship is etymological, not a claim about what James meant."
